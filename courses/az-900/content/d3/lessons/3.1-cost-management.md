## What drives your Azure bill

Azure bills for what you consume, so cost is the product of several independent factors. Know these seven; the exam likes to ask which factor explains a given difference.

| Factor | What it means |
|---|---|
| Resource type | Each service has its own meters (compute hours, GB stored, requests, transactions). Size, tier and SKU change the rate. |
| Consumption | How much you use. Pay-as-you-go charges for actual use; reservations and savings plans trade a commitment for a discount. |
| Maintenance | Resources you leave running (or allocated) still cost money. A stopped-but-allocated resource can keep billing for some parts. |
| Geography | The same resource has different rates in different regions (power, labour and taxes vary). |
| Network traffic | Data entering Azure is free. Data leaving a region or Azure to the internet (egress) is billed after a free allowance. |
| Subscription type | Pay-as-you-go, Enterprise Agreement, CSP, free account and dev/test offers carry different terms and discounts. |
| Azure Marketplace | Third-party and partner products add their own charges on your Azure invoice. |

Beyond these, the **support plan** you buy and **licensing** choices matter. **Azure Hybrid Benefit** lets you reuse eligible on-premises Windows Server or SQL Server licences (with Software Assurance or qualifying subscriptions) to cut the licence portion of the bill. Reservations, savings plans and Spot pricing were covered in domain 1.

> **Exam tip:** "Moving data out of Azure" costs money; "moving data in" does not. A question about two identical VMs priced differently usually points to geography (region) or pricing model.

## The Azure pricing calculator

The **Azure pricing calculator** is a free, web-based tool that **estimates** the monthly cost of a solution you have not built yet. You pick products (Virtual Machines, SQL Database, storage...), set region, tier, size, quantity and expected hours, and the estimate updates like a shopping cart. You can choose pay-as-you-go, one- or three-year reservations or savings plans where offered, add a support plan, then **export** (to Excel) or **share** the estimate. Anyone can use it without signing in; if you sign in with a billing account you can see your negotiated prices.

Calculator figures are estimates. Real bills depend on actual usage, so compare with Cost Management afterwards.

### TCO calculator: check the current state

Older course material pairs the pricing calculator with the **Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) calculator**, which compared on-premises cost (hardware, electricity, IT labour, datacenter space) with Azure. Microsoft's current AZ-900 outline (skills as of July 20, 2026) lists only "explore the pricing calculator", and the old TCO calculator web address now redirects away from the calculator. Microsoft's current route for an on-premises-versus-Azure comparison is the **Azure Migrate business case** feature, which models total cost of ownership for discovered servers. Treat the TCO calculator as legacy: you may still see it named in older questions, where its job is "compare on-premises to Azure cost", while the pricing calculator is "estimate the cost of Azure services".

| Question | Tool |
|---|---|
| What will these Azure resources cost per month? | Pricing calculator |
| What am I actually spending now, and where? | Microsoft Cost Management |
| What would migrating my datacenter save? | Azure Migrate business case (legacy: TCO calculator) |

## Microsoft Cost Management

**Microsoft Cost Management** (part of Cost Management + Billing) helps you monitor, allocate and optimise spend after resources exist. Capabilities to know:

- **Cost analysis:** interactive charts of costs by subscription, resource group, resource, service, location or **tag**; view accumulated or daily cost, and a forecast.
- **Budgets:** a spending threshold at a scope (management group, subscription, resource group). You choose a period (monthly, quarterly, annually) and alert thresholds.
- **Alerts:** budget alerts fire at percentages of a budget (actual or forecasted). Other alert types include credit, department spending quota and anomaly alerts. Alerts can email people or call an **action group** (for example to run automation).
- **Exports:** scheduled export of cost data to a storage account for reporting.
- **Recommendations:** cost-saving advice is delivered through Azure Advisor (right-size or shut down idle VMs, buy reservations).
- **Cost allocation:** split shared costs and group costs by tag so teams see their own spend.

> **Watch out:** A budget **does not stop spending**. It notifies you (and can trigger automation you build). Nothing is shut down by the budget itself.

Access to cost data is governed by RBAC (for example Cost Management Reader), so being able to see a resource does not automatically let you see its cost.

## Tags

A **tag** is a name/value pair attached to a resource, resource group or subscription, such as `Environment = Prod` or `CostCenter = 4417`. Tags are metadata: they do not change how a resource works. Their main uses are:

- **Cost allocation:** filter and group in Cost Management by tag to charge back departments.
- **Organisation and automation:** find all resources for a project, owner or lifecycle stage; target automation.
- **Governance:** Azure Policy can require or add tags.

The facts the exam tests:

1. **Tags are not inherited by default.** Tagging a resource group does *not* tag its resources, and tagging a subscription does not tag its resource groups.
2. To get inheritance, use **Azure Policy**: built-in definitions such as "Inherit a tag from the resource group" (effect `modify`) copy a tag onto resources, and "Require a tag on resources" (effect `deny`) blocks creation without it. Existing resources are fixed by a remediation task.
3. Each tag is a key and a value; keys must be unique on a resource. Not every resource type supports tags, and there are limits on the count and length (look them up rather than memorising).
4. A tag on a resource group alone does not appear on that group's cost lines unless the resources carry the tag too.
5. To apply a tag you need write access to the resource (for example Contributor) or the **Tag Contributor** role, which allows tagging without access to the resource itself.

> **Exam tip:** "Show Finance how much each department spends" = tags plus Cost Management cost analysis. "Make sure every new resource has a CostCenter tag" = Azure Policy, not another tag.

## Try it in the portal

With a free account (nothing here costs money):

1. Open the pricing calculator in a browser, add a Virtual Machine, change the region and then the tier, and watch the estimate change. Add a support plan and export the estimate.
2. In the portal search for **Cost Management**, open **Cost analysis** and group by service, then by tag (if you have none, the group is "untagged").
3. Under **Budgets**, create a small monthly budget on your subscription with an alert at 80 percent to your email.
4. Open any resource group, choose **Tags**, add `Environment = Lab`, then open a resource inside it and confirm the tag is *not* there.

## Key takeaways

- Cost factors: resource type, consumption, maintenance, geography, network traffic (egress costs, ingress is free), subscription type and Marketplace.
- The pricing calculator estimates the cost of Azure services before deployment; it is free and needs no sign-in.
- The TCO calculator is legacy; the current on-premises-versus-Azure comparison is the Azure Migrate business case.
- Cost Management analyses actual spend, sets budgets, sends alerts and exports data.
- A budget alerts and can trigger automation, but never stops resources by itself.
- Tags are name/value metadata used for cost allocation and organisation.
- Tags are not inherited; Azure Policy (modify or deny effects) can copy or require them.
