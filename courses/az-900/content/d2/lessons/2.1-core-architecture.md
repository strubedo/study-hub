## Regions and geographies

An Azure **region** is a geographic perimeter containing one or more datacenters connected by a low-latency network. Each region sits inside a **geography**, which is a fixed data-residency boundary. When a residency rule says "data must stay in the EU", you pick regions in the matching geography. Not every service is offered in every region, and prices and features can differ by region, so the region you pick affects latency, compliance, available services and cost.

### Region pairs and nonpaired regions

Microsoft pairs many regions (for example East US with West US, North Europe with West Europe). You cannot choose or change a pair. Facts worth knowing:

- Pairs are usually in the same geography, normally separated by a long distance (typically hundreds of miles) so that one disaster does not hit both.
- Planned platform updates are staggered, so only one region of a pair is updated at a time, and during a geography-wide outage one region of each pair is prioritized for recovery.
- Geo-redundant storage (GRS, GZRS) replicates to the paired region automatically. For most other services you must deploy your own copy in a second region.
- Some pairs are asymmetrical. Brazil South is paired with South Central US, but South Central US is not paired back with Brazil South, and Brazil South is the documented exception to "pairs stay in one geography".
- Many newer regions (for example Chile Central, Mexico Central, Austria East, Belgium Central, Denmark East, Italy North, Poland Central, Spain Central, Israel Central, Qatar Central, Indonesia Central, Malaysia West, New Zealand North) are **nonpaired**. They rely on availability zones for resilience, and many services still support geo-replication to any chosen region.

> **Exam tip:** A region pair gives you no automatic failover for your own resources. It is a Microsoft relationship used by some services (notably GRS storage) and by staged maintenance. You are still responsible for designing your own disaster recovery.

### Sovereign regions

Sovereign clouds are isolated Azure instances for specific legal or compliance needs. They are separate from the public cloud, with their own portal, endpoints and identity, and they often offer fewer services.

- **Azure Government** serves US federal, state, local and tribal government and their partners. Regions include US Gov Virginia, US Gov Arizona and US Gov Texas, plus US DoD Central and US DoD East reserved for the Department of Defense.
- **Azure operated by 21Vianet (Azure China)** is operated in China by a local partner, with regions such as China East 3 and China North 3.

## Availability zones and datacenters

An **availability zone** is a separate group of one or more datacenters within a region, with independent power, cooling and networking. Zones are close enough for low-latency links but far enough apart that one local event (storm, power loss) is unlikely to hit more than one. Key facts:

- Every region that supports availability zones has **at least three** zones. Some have more.
- Not every region has zones, and not every service supports them in every region.
- A **datacenter** is a physical facility; a zone is a logical grouping of one or more of them. A single datacenter alone gives you no zone-level protection.
- **Zonal** resources are pinned to one zone by you (for example a VM in zone 2); you handle failover between zones. **Zone-redundant** resources are spread across zones and Microsoft handles replication and failover (for example ZRS storage, zone-redundant gateways).
- Zones protect against datacenter-level failures inside a region. Region pairs and multi-region designs protect against a whole region failing.

| Failure | Protect with |
|---|---|
| Server rack or host | Availability set or fault domains |
| Datacenter or zone outage | Availability zones |
| Whole region outage | Multiple regions (region pair or any second region) |

## Resources and resource groups

A **resource** is a manageable item: a VM, storage account, virtual network, web app. A **resource group** is a logical container that holds related resources for an Azure solution. Rules to remember:

- Every resource belongs to **exactly one** resource group, and a resource group cannot be nested inside another.
- A resource group stores metadata, so its location can differ from the location of the resources inside it.
- Resources in a group typically share a lifecycle: deploy, update and delete together. Deleting a resource group deletes everything in it.
- Access (RBAC), policy and locks applied to a resource group are inherited by the resources inside it. Tags are not inherited by default.
- Resources can be moved between resource groups (and subscriptions) for many resource types.
- Resources in different groups can still talk to each other (for example, a VM in `rg-web-prod` can use a virtual network in `rg-net-prod`).

## Subscriptions and management groups

A **subscription** is a billing and access boundary and a unit of scale. It is linked to one Microsoft Entra tenant, and all resource usage in it is billed together (you can have several subscriptions for separate environments, departments or billing). Subscriptions also carry quotas and service limits.

**Management groups** are containers above subscriptions that let you apply Azure Policy and Azure RBAC to many subscriptions at once through inheritance.

- A directory has one built-in **root (tenant root) management group**; every other management group and subscription folds up to it. New subscriptions land under the root by default.
- A management group tree supports up to **six levels of depth**, not counting the root level or the subscription level.
- Each management group and each subscription has only **one parent**; a management group can have many children.
- A directory can hold up to 10,000 management groups.
- All subscriptions in a management group must trust the same Microsoft Entra tenant.

## The hierarchy

```text
Tenant root management group
  └─ Management group (up to 6 levels below root)
       └─ Subscription
            └─ Resource group
                 └─ Resource
```

Policy assignments and role assignments flow **down** this tree. A role granted on a management group applies to every subscription, resource group and resource beneath it. A resource or subscription owner cannot alter a policy that is assigned above them.

> **Watch out:** Resource groups are not nested, and a resource has only one resource group. If an exam option says "a resource group inside another resource group" or "a resource in two resource groups", it is wrong.

| Scope | Typical use |
|---|---|
| Management group | Policy and access across many subscriptions |
| Subscription | Billing, quotas, environment separation (Contoso-Prod, Contoso-Dev) |
| Resource group | Lifecycle grouping of related resources |
| Resource | The actual service instance |

## Try it in the portal

With a free account:

1. Search **Resource groups**, select **Create**, and create `rg-lab-eastus` in East US. Note the **Region** field belongs to the group's metadata.
2. Search **Subscriptions** and open yours; note the subscription ID and the directory (tenant) it belongs to.
3. Search **Management groups** and open it; you will see the tenant root group. Create a child group and drag your subscription under it to see the hierarchy.
4. Create a storage account in the new group, then delete the resource group and watch everything in it go.

## Key takeaways

- Regions sit inside geographies, which are data-residency boundaries; pairs cannot be chosen and newer regions are often nonpaired.
- Sovereign clouds (Azure Government, Azure operated by 21Vianet) are isolated from public Azure.
- Regions with availability zones have at least three; zones protect against datacenter failure, pairs and multi-region designs against region failure.
- A resource lives in exactly one resource group, and resource groups do not nest.
- A subscription is a billing and access boundary tied to one Microsoft Entra tenant.
- Management groups allow six levels of depth excluding the root and subscription levels; each item has one parent.
- Hierarchy: management group, subscription, resource group, resource; policy and RBAC inherit downward.
