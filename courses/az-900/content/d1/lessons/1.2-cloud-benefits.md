## High availability

**Availability** is the proportion of time a service is up and usable. **High availability** (HA) means designing so that a failure of one component does not take the service down. In Azure this rests on redundancy at several levels: multiple instances, spreading across fault domains, availability zones within a region, and multiple regions. Providers back availability with a **service-level agreement (SLA)**, a formal commitment to a percentage of uptime, often expressed in "nines" (a higher number of nines means less permitted downtime). Combining services affects the overall availability of a solution: a chain of dependent components is only as available as the combined SLAs allow, and redundancy raises it.

> **Exam tip:** Service-level agreement = the provider's uptime commitment. A customer-chosen design with extra redundancy (zones, regions) can improve effective availability beyond a single instance.

## Scalability

**Scalability** is the ability to adjust resources to meet demand.

- **Vertical scaling (scale up/down):** change the size of an existing instance, for example more CPU and memory on a VM.
- **Horizontal scaling (scale out/in):** add or remove instances, for example more web servers behind a load balancer.
- **Autoscaling:** rules or metrics add or remove capacity automatically (Virtual Machine Scale Sets and App Service support this).

**Elasticity** is scaling that follows demand closely in both directions, so you do not pay for idle capacity after a peak. Scalability is the capability; elasticity is its dynamic use.

| Need | Concept |
|---|---|
| Bigger VM for one heavy process | Vertical scaling |
| More VMs for more users | Horizontal scaling |
| Capacity follows traffic without manual action | Autoscale / elasticity |

## Reliability

**Reliability** is the ability of a system to recover from failures and keep functioning. The cloud supports it through a global footprint of datacenters and regions, redundancy, automatic failover, backup and disaster recovery services, and replication of data across zones or regions. A reliable design assumes components will fail and keeps the workload running or restores it quickly.

> **Watch out:** Reliability (recovering from failure) and availability (being up) overlap but are different words. Scalability (matching demand) is a separate benefit from both.

## Predictability

Predictability has two forms:

- **Performance predictability:** the workload behaves consistently under load because autoscaling and load balancing keep capacity matched to demand, and because built-in diagnostics help you meet performance targets.
- **Cost predictability:** you can forecast and control spend. Tools include the **pricing calculator** for estimates, and Cost Management for tracking and budgets. Consumption-based billing makes cost track usage; reservations and savings plans give more certainty for steady workloads.

## Security

Cloud providers invest in security at a scale few organizations match: physical datacenter protection, hardened infrastructure, threat intelligence, and encryption options. Customers also get tooling to secure what they own: identity and access controls, network security, encryption, and monitoring such as Microsoft Defender for Cloud. Remember the shared responsibility model: the provider secures the cloud infrastructure, the customer still secures data, identities and devices, and configures the services correctly.

## Governance

Governance is the set of policies and controls that keep resources consistent, compliant and cost-controlled. Cloud governance means you can **enforce standards automatically**: for instance, require specific regions or tags, block expensive SKUs, audit configuration against a compliance baseline, and lock critical resources. Azure tools (covered in domain 3) include Azure Policy, resource locks and role-based access control. Providers also certify their datacenters against many industry and regional compliance standards and publish the audit reports, so customers can inherit part of their own compliance posture.

## Manageability

Manageability has two sides:

- **Management *of* the cloud:** autoscaling, deploying from templates or code, monitoring and alerting, and automated capacity management.
- **Management *in* the cloud:** the ways you operate resources: the web portal, command-line tools (Azure CLI, Azure PowerShell), REST APIs, and infrastructure as code.

Together these let you deploy repeatably, monitor health, and automate responses, instead of managing individual servers by hand.

## Matching scenarios to benefits

| Scenario | Benefit |
|---|---|
| Web store adds servers during a sale and removes them afterward | Scalability / elasticity |
| Application keeps running when one datacenter in a region fails | High availability |
| Service restores from a replica in another region after an outage | Reliability |
| Finance team can forecast next quarter's compute spend | Predictability (cost) |
| Policies automatically reject deployments in non-approved regions | Governance |
| Deploying 50 identical environments from one template | Manageability |
| Provider's physical datacenter certifications | Security / compliance |

> **Exam tip:** Questions here are scenario-to-benefit matches. Identify the verb: "add or remove capacity" = scalability; "stay running despite a failure" = high availability; "recover" = reliability; "forecast" = predictability; "enforce standards" = governance; "deploy or monitor with automation" = manageability.

## Try it in the portal

In the portal, open **Virtual machine scale sets** (or an App Service plan) and look at the **Scaling** blade to see manual versus autoscale rules. In **Policy**, browse **Definitions** to see built-in governance rules such as allowed locations. Open **Advisor** to see the reliability, security, cost and performance recommendation categories. You do not need to deploy anything to see these screens.

## Key takeaways

- High availability = designing so a failure does not stop the service; an SLA is the provider's uptime commitment.
- Scalability adjusts resources to demand: vertical (bigger) or horizontal (more); autoscaling and elasticity do it automatically.
- Reliability is recovering from failure; the cloud's redundancy and global footprint support it.
- Predictability covers both performance and cost.
- Security is shared: the provider secures the infrastructure, the customer secures data, identities and configuration.
- Governance means enforcing standards and compliance with policy and controls.
- Manageability covers management of the cloud (autoscale, templates, monitoring) and in the cloud (portal, CLI, APIs).
