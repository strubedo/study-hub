## Two limits on how much a sender may have in flight

A TCP sender may have unacknowledged data outstanding only up to the **minimum of two windows**:

- The **receive window (rwnd)**, advertised by the receiver in every segment's Window field. This is **flow control**: protecting the receiver's buffer.
- The **congestion window (cwnd)**, calculated by the sender from loss and delay signals. This is **congestion control**: protecting the network.

`bytes in flight <= min(rwnd, cwnd)`

Throughput is bounded by window divided by round-trip time, which gives the bandwidth-delay product (BDP) rule: to fill a path you need a window at least as big as `bandwidth x RTT`. A 1 Gbit/s path with 40 ms RTT needs 1,000,000,000 / 8 x 0.04 = 5,000,000 bytes in flight.

## Sliding window and flow control

The sender keeps a window of sequence numbers it may send. As ACKs arrive the window slides forward, so the pipe stays full instead of stop-and-wait. The receiver's advertised window shrinks as its buffer fills and grows as the application reads. Related behaviours:

- **Zero window.** If rwnd reaches 0, the sender stops and starts a **persist timer**, sending small window probes until a window update arrives. Wireshark flags `TCP ZeroWindow`, `TCP Window Update` and `TCP Window Full` (the sender filled the advertised window). Repeated zero windows point to a slow receiving application, not a network fault.
- **Delayed ACK.** A receiver may wait to acknowledge, but must do so within 0.5 seconds and must acknowledge at least every second full-sized segment (RFC 9293). This is why acknowledgments often cover two segments.
- **Nagle's algorithm** delays sending small segments while earlier data is unacknowledged, which helps efficiency but can cause 40-200 ms stalls with delayed ACK; interactive applications disable it with `TCP_NODELAY`.

## Window scaling

The Window field is 16 bits, so the largest unscaled window is 65,535 bytes. At a 40 ms RTT that caps throughput at 65,535 x 8 / 0.04 = about 13.1 Mbit/s, no matter how fast the link. The **window scale** option (RFC 7323, kind 3) multiplies the advertised value by 2^S, where S is the shift count in the SYN:

- Each side announces its own shift (0-14) in its SYN and in its SYN-ACK. The option appears **only in SYN segments**, and only takes effect if **both** sides sent it.
- The maximum shift is 14, giving a maximum window of 65,535 x 2^14 = 1,073,725,440 bytes (a little under 1 GiB).
- The Window field in a SYN or SYN-ACK itself is never scaled.
- A middlebox that strips the option collapses throughput to the 64 KB limit while the capture still looks healthy. The check: look at the SYN for `WS=` and whether the SYN-ACK echoes it.

Linux auto-tunes receive buffers via `net.ipv4.tcp_rmem` (min, default, max) and window scaling is on by default (`tcp_window_scaling = 1`).

## Retransmission

TCP detects loss in two ways.

**Retransmission timeout (RTO).** The sender times each segment. RFC 6298 computes `RTO = SRTT + max(G, 4 x RTTVAR)` from smoothed round-trip samples, keeps it **at least 1 second** (Linux deviates: its minimum RTO is 200 ms), starts at **1 second** before any measurement, and **doubles it after each timeout** (exponential backoff). **Karn's algorithm** forbids using an RTT sample from a retransmitted segment, since the ACK could belong to either copy. A timeout is a severe signal: the sender collapses cwnd to one segment and re-enters slow start.

**Duplicate ACKs and fast retransmit.** When a segment is lost but later ones arrive, the receiver cannot advance its cumulative ACK, so it repeats the same ACK number for each out-of-order arrival. A duplicate ACK (RFC 5681) is one with the same acknowledgment number, no data, no window change and no SYN/FIN, while data is outstanding. After **three duplicate ACKs** (that is, the original ACK plus three repeats = four identical ACKs) the sender retransmits the missing segment immediately without waiting for the RTO. That is **fast retransmit**.

```text
No.  Time   Source        Destination   Info
101  0.100  198.51.100.20 192.0.2.10    443 > 44312 [ACK] Seq=1 Ack=2897 Win=501
102  0.101  198.51.100.20 192.0.2.10    [TCP Dup ACK 101#1] 443 > 44312 [ACK] Seq=1 Ack=2897 SLE=4345 SRE=5793
103  0.101  198.51.100.20 192.0.2.10    [TCP Dup ACK 101#2] 443 > 44312 [ACK] Seq=1 Ack=2897 SLE=4345 SRE=7241
104  0.102  198.51.100.20 192.0.2.10    [TCP Dup ACK 101#3] 443 > 44312 [ACK] Seq=1 Ack=2897 SLE=4345 SRE=8689
105  0.102  192.0.2.10    198.51.100.20 [TCP Fast Retransmission] 44312 > 443 Seq=2897 Len=1448
```

Here the receiver has everything up to byte 2896 (Ack=2897), but is also holding bytes 4345-8688 (the SLE/SRE pairs are the left and right edges of a SACK block). Segment 2897-4344 is missing and is retransmitted without a timeout. The filters `tcp.analysis.retransmission`, `tcp.analysis.fast_retransmission`, `tcp.analysis.duplicate_ack` and `tcp.analysis.lost_segment` find these.

### SACK

Cumulative ACKs say only "I have everything before X". **Selective acknowledgment** (RFC 2018) lets the receiver report non-contiguous blocks it holds. SACK-permitted (kind 4) is exchanged in the SYNs, and SACK blocks (kind 5) ride in later ACKs, 8 bytes per block, at most 4 blocks per segment (3 when the timestamps option is present). The sender retransmits only the holes, which is far more efficient when several segments in a window are lost. Linux enables it by default (`tcp_sack = 1`).

### Timestamps

The timestamps option (kind 8, 10 bytes, usually padded to 12) carries TSval and TSecr, which give accurate RTT samples even for retransmissions and provide **PAWS** (protection against wrapped sequence numbers), which matters once windows reach gigabit scale. It is negotiated in the SYNs.

## Congestion control

Congestion control (RFC 5681) assumes that loss indicates congestion, and probes for bandwidth in two phases:

| Phase | When | cwnd growth |
|---|---|---|
| **Slow start** | cwnd < ssthresh | +1 SMSS per ACK received, so cwnd roughly **doubles each RTT** |
| **Congestion avoidance** | cwnd >= ssthresh | About +1 SMSS per RTT (cwnd += SMSS x SMSS / cwnd per ACK), linear growth |

The **initial window** was 2-4 segments in RFC 5681 and was raised to **10 segments** (RFC 6928, IW10), which Linux uses. The initial ssthresh is arbitrarily high so slow start continues until the first loss.

How the sender reacts to loss:

| Event | ssthresh | cwnd |
|---|---|---|
| Retransmission timeout | max(FlightSize / 2, 2 x SMSS) | 1 SMSS (back to slow start) |
| 3 duplicate ACKs (**fast recovery**) | max(FlightSize / 2, 2 x SMSS) | ssthresh + 3 x SMSS, then inflates by 1 SMSS per additional duplicate ACK and deflates to ssthresh when new data is acknowledged, then congestion avoidance |

Fast recovery halves the rate rather than collapsing it, because duplicate ACKs prove that packets are still getting through. The classic result is the AIMD sawtooth (additive increase, multiplicative decrease). Algorithms beyond Reno change the details: Linux defaults to **CUBIC** (`sysctl net.ipv4.tcp_congestion_control`), and BBR models bandwidth and delay instead of reacting to loss.

### ECN

**Explicit Congestion Notification** (RFC 3168) lets a router signal congestion by marking instead of dropping. It uses two IP bits (Not-ECT 00, ECT(1) 01, ECT(0) 10, CE 11) and two TCP flags (ECE, CWR):

1. **Negotiation.** The client's SYN carries ECE and CWR; a server that supports ECN answers the SYN-ACK with ECE set and CWR clear. Both hosts then send data with ECT(0) or ECT(1) in the IP header.
2. A router with an AQM (active queue management) policy sets **CE** (11) on an ECT packet instead of dropping it when its queue builds.
3. The receiver sets **ECE** on its ACKs until it sees CWR.
4. The sender halves its congestion window as for a loss (once per window of data, without retransmitting) and sets **CWR** on the next new data segment.

Marked packets are delivered, so ECN avoids both the loss and the retransmission delay. On Linux `net.ipv4.tcp_ecn` defaults to 2 (accept ECN when the peer requests it but don't request it on outgoing connections); 1 requests it too, 0 disables it. A device that blocks SYNs carrying ECE/CWR makes ECN-enabled connections fail, which is a known historic middlebox bug.

> **Exam tip:** Slow start is exponential, congestion avoidance is linear, a timeout resets cwnd to 1 SMSS, three duplicate ACKs halve it. Flow control is the receiver's window; congestion control is the sender's cwnd.

## Lab it yourself

1. Look at live state: `ss -tin dst 198.51.100.20` shows `cubic`, `wscale:7,7`, `rto:204`, `rtt`, `mss`, `cwnd:10`, `ssthresh`, `retrans`, and SACK/ECN markers.
2. Induce loss with `tc`: `sudo tc qdisc add dev eth0 root netem loss 2%` (remove with `tc qdisc del dev eth0 root`), run `iperf3 -c <server>`, and watch `tcp.analysis.retransmission` and `tcp.analysis.duplicate_ack` count up. Plot **Statistics > TCP Stream Graphs > Time Sequence (Stevens)** and **Window Scaling**.
3. Compare window scaling on and off: `sudo sysctl -w net.ipv4.tcp_window_scaling=0` and repeat a transfer over `netem delay 40ms`; throughput should cap near 13 Mbit/s. Restore it to 1 afterwards.
4. Inspect the SYN options in Wireshark and note MSS, WS, SACK_PERM and TS. `sysctl net.ipv4.tcp_ecn` on your machine; set it to 1 and look for ECE|CWR in the SYN.
5. Cisco IOS: `show tcp statistics`, `show tcp brief`; global knobs include `ip tcp window-size`, `ip tcp selective-ack` and `ip tcp timestamp`.

## Key takeaways

- In-flight data is limited to min(rwnd, cwnd): flow control (receiver) and congestion control (sender).
- Maximum unscaled window 65,535 bytes; window scale (SYN only, both sides, shift 0-14) permits up to about 1 GiB.
- Throughput <= window / RTT; fill a path with a window of at least bandwidth x RTT.
- RTO starts at 1 s, is derived from SRTT/RTTVAR, backs off exponentially, and follows Karn's rule; Linux's minimum is 200 ms.
- Three duplicate ACKs trigger fast retransmit; SACK (negotiated in SYNs) reports out-of-order blocks so only holes are resent.
- Slow start doubles cwnd per RTT up to ssthresh, then congestion avoidance adds about one segment per RTT. Timeout resets cwnd to one segment; fast recovery halves it.
- ECN marks (CE) instead of dropping; ECE echoes it, CWR acknowledges the reduction; negotiated with ECE+CWR on the SYN and ECE on the SYN-ACK.
