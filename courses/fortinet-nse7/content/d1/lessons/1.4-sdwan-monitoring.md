## What "SD-WAN is working" means

Monitoring answers four questions, and each has its own tool:

| Question | GUI | CLI |
|----------|-----|-----|
| Are the members alive and in SLA? | Network > SD-WAN > Performance SLAs | `diagnose sys sdwan health-check`, `diagnose sys sdwan sla-log` |
| How is traffic spread over members? | Network > SD-WAN > SD-WAN Zones (Bandwidth, Volume, Sessions) | `diagnose sys sdwan member`, `diagnose sys sdwan intf-sla-log` |
| Which member does each rule prefer, and why? | Network > SD-WAN > SD-WAN Rules (selected member and hover reason) | `diagnose sys sdwan service4`, `diagnose firewall proute list` |
| What changed and when? | Log & Report > Events > SD-WAN events; Dashboard widgets; FortiAnalyzer | Event logs `subtype="sdwan"` and system "Routing information changed" logs |

## Member health

```text
FGT-Spoke1 # diagnose sys sdwan health-check Internet
Health Check(Internet):
Seq(1 port1): state(alive), packet-loss(0.000%) latency(18.402), jitter(1.215) sla_map=0x1
Seq(2 port2): state(dead), packet-loss(100.000%) sla_map=0x0
```

- `state(alive|dead)` is **link status**: the member is dead after `failtime` consecutive failed probes (default 5) and alive again after `recoverytime` successes (default 5), probing every `interval` (default 500 ms; HTTP and HTTPS probes are automatically stretched to 120 000 ms). With the defaults, a dead link is detected in about 2.5 seconds.
- `sla_map` is a bitmap of the SLA targets the member currently meets: bit 0 (0x1) is target 1, bit 1 (0x2) target 2. `sla_map=0x0` on an alive member means it is up but **out of SLA**.
- Latency and jitter targets are evaluated over the last 30 probes and packet loss over the last 100 probes.
- With two servers in a health check, FortiOS probes the first and uses the second only when the first is unavailable; the check fails only when both are unreachable.
- If a member has several health checks, all of them must fail before its routes are removed.

When a member goes dead and `update-static-route` is enabled, FortiOS withdraws its static routes and logs it:

```text
date=2026-10-29 time=09:14:02 logid="0100022921" type="event" subtype="system" level="critical" vd="root" logdesc="Routing information changed" name="Internet" interface="port2" status="down" msg="Static route on interface port2 may be removed by health-check Internet. Route: (198.51.100.2->1.1.1.1 ping-down)"
```

The 10-minute history is in `diagnose sys sdwan sla-log <health-check> <member-seq>`, and `diagnose sys sdwan intf-sla-log <interface>` keeps 15 minutes of per-interface bandwidth samples.

## Traffic distribution

### Implicit rule load-balance modes

| `load-balance-mode` | GUI name | Spreads new sessions by |
|---------------------|----------|-------------------------|
| `source-ip-based` (default) | Source IP | Hash of source IP; all of a host's sessions use one member |
| `source-dest-ip-based` | Source-Destination IP | Hash of source and destination IP |
| `weight-based` | Sessions | Session ratio from each member's `weight` (1–255) |
| `measured-volume-based` | Volume | Byte ratio from each member's `volume-ratio` |
| `usage-based` | Spillover | Fills the first member up to its `spillover-threshold` (egress) / `ingress-spillover-threshold`, then spills to the next |

All of them only spread traffic over members that have a **best route** to the destination. If one member's default route has a higher distance or priority, the implicit rule cannot use it.

```text
FGT-Spoke1 # diagnose sys sdwan member
Member(1): interface: port1, flags=0x0 , gateway: 192.0.2.1, priority: 1 1024, weight: 33
  Session count: 412
Member(2): interface: port2, flags=0x0 , gateway: 198.51.100.1, priority: 1 1024, weight: 66
  Session count: 851
```

With `weight-based` and weights 1:2, the session counts should approach a one-to-two ratio. In `source-ip-based` and `source-dest-ip-based` modes the same command shows `weight: 0` and no session counters. With `usage-based`, the member output shows `Egress-spillover-threshold`, `ingress-spillover-threshold` and `Egress-overbps=1` when a member is over its limit; `diagnose netlink dstmac list <interface>` shows the same over-limit flags. Event logs record a member entering "conservative status" when it is over its configured ratio and resuming afterwards.

### Rules and the members they select

```text
FGT-Spoke1 # diagnose sys sdwan service4
Service(1): Address Mode(IPV4) flags=0x200
  Gen(3), TOS(0x0/0x0), Protocol(0: 1->65535), Mode(sla), sla-compare-order
  Members(2):
    1: Seq_num(1 port1), alive, sla(0x1), gid(0), cfg_order(0), cost(0), selected
    2: Seq_num(2 port2), alive, sla(0x0), gid(0), cfg_order(1), cost(0), selected
  Src address(1):
        10.1.100.0-10.1.100.255
  Dst address(1):
        0.0.0.0-255.255.255.255
```

- The mode line tells you the strategy (`manual`, `priority` for best quality with its `link-cost-factor` and `link-cost-threshold`, `sla` for lowest cost, `load-balance`).
- Members are listed in preference order and new sessions go to the first usable one. `selected` marks every member the rule can use, so an alive but out-of-SLA member is still `selected`, listed after the members that meet the SLA, as a fallback; a dead member is not selected.
- A rule whose members have **no valid route** to the destination is skipped (inactive), and traffic falls to the next rule or the implicit rule.
- Each SD-WAN rule is installed as a policy route: `diagnose firewall proute list` shows `vwl_service=<id>(<name>)`, the member sequence (`vwl_mbr_seq`), outgoing interfaces and a hit count.

Per-session proof: in `diagnose sys session list` or FortiView Sessions, the destination interface shows which member a session used. Remember that established sessions keep their member until something triggers re-evaluation (routing change, member failure); Domain 4 covers session re-evaluation in detail.

## SD-WAN pages and widgets

| Where | What you see |
|-------|--------------|
| Network > SD-WAN > SD-WAN Zones | Members per zone; charts for **Bandwidth** (upload/download per interface), **Volume** (donut of sent and received bytes) and **Sessions** (donut of active sessions per member) |
| Network > SD-WAN > Performance SLAs | Per health check: live packet loss, latency and jitter per member, the server in use, thresholds, and a green/red arrow for alive/dead (green means responding, not "meets the SLA") |
| Network > SD-WAN > SD-WAN Rules | Each rule's members, the selected member (check mark) and, on hover, why it was selected |
| Dashboard > Network | The **SD-WAN** widget in the Network category, plus Interface Bandwidth (up to 25 interfaces) and Routing (Static & Dynamic) for the routes SD-WAN installs |
| FortiManager / FortiAnalyzer | SD-WAN Monitor (map and table views, per-device SLA graphs), FortiView SD-WAN / Secure SD-WAN Monitor widgets for many sites |

Most widgets can be saved as a standalone monitor (expand to full screen, *Save as Monitor*); full screen is not supported by every widget.

## SD-WAN logs and events

SD-WAN events use `type="event" subtype="sdwan"`:

| logid | logdesc | Raised when |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| 0113022923 | SDWAN status | Health check SLA membership changes ("Number of pass member changed", "Member out-of-sla", "Member in sla"); a rule's selected member changes ("Service prioritized by SLA will be redirected in sequence order", "...by performance metric..."); a member stops or starts forwarding ("Member link is unreachable or miss threshold. Stop forwarding traffic.") |
| 0113022924 | SDWAN volume status | A member enters or leaves conservative status in volume/session load balancing |
| 0113022925 | SDWAN SLA information | Periodic SLA status, every `sla-fail-log-period` while failing and `sla-pass-log-period` while passing; carries latency, jitter, packetloss, bandwidth used/available, `slamap` and, on failure, the failing `metric` |

Link up/down from a health check is also logged as a **system** event (logid 0100022921, "Routing information changed"), because what actually happens is a static route being removed or added.

```text
FGT-Spoke1 # config system sdwan
FGT-Spoke1 (sdwan) # config health-check
FGT-Spoke1 (health-check) # edit "Internet"
FGT-Spoke1 (Internet) # set sla-fail-log-period 30
FGT-Spoke1 (Internet) # set sla-pass-log-period 300
FGT-Spoke1 (Internet) # end
FGT-Spoke1 (sdwan) # end
```

Traffic logs complete the picture: with `logtraffic all` on the SD-WAN policy, each forward traffic log shows the destination interface (the member actually used), which is how you prove that a rule steered a given application. FortiAnalyzer uses the periodic SLA logs to build SD-WAN reports and dashboards, so enabling the pass and fail log periods matters for long-term trending.

> **Exam tip:** "The health check is green but the rule still sends traffic over the other link" — green only means the probe server answers. Check `sla_map`: the member may be alive but out of SLA.

> **Watch out:** An SLA log with `status="up"` and `slamap="0x0"` is not a contradiction. The link is up (alive), but it fails the SLA target named in `metric`.

## Lab it yourself

Use the DIA lab from the previous lesson (trial VM, two Cisco "ISPs", one policy, one route). Then:

- Run `diagnose sys sdwan health-check` while adding latency on one path (for example `tc qdisc add dev eth1 root netem delay 200ms` on a Linux router in the path) and watch `sla_map` drop to 0x0 while the state stays alive.
- Change `load-balance-mode` to `weight-based` with weights 1 and 2, generate traffic from several hosts or source IPs, and compare session counts in `diagnose sys sdwan member`.
- Shut one Cisco ISP interface and find the "Routing information changed" system event and the SD-WAN status events in Log & Report.

All of this stays within the trial's three interfaces, three policies and three routes. FortiManager SD-WAN Monitor needs the FortiManager-VM trial (three devices, two ADOMs) and FortiAnalyzer reports need a FortiAnalyzer.

## Key takeaways

- `diagnose sys sdwan health-check`: state alive/dead is link status; sla_map shows which SLA targets are met.
- Defaults: interval 500 ms, failtime 5, recoverytime 5; latency and jitter over 30 probes, loss over 100.
- The implicit rule balances by source IP by default; weight, volume, source-destination and spillover modes exist, but only across members with equal best routes.
- `diagnose sys sdwan service4` shows each rule's mode and selected members; rules without a valid route are skipped.
- SD-WAN Zones charts show bandwidth, volume and sessions per member; the Performance SLAs page shows loss, latency and jitter per health check.
- SD-WAN events: 0113022923 status changes, 0113022924 volume status, 0113022925 periodic SLA information; member route withdrawals appear as system event 0100022921.
