## Multiregion topologies and large deployments

As the site count grows across continents, one hub pair stops being practical: latency to a distant hub, blast radius, BGP table size. Fortinet's 7.6 SD-WAN architecture guide then defines **regions**:

- Each region is an ordinary single- or dual-hub hub-and-spoke (for example `FGT-Hub-EU`, `FGT-Hub-US`).
- Spokes connect **only** to their regional hubs, both for IPsec overlays and for BGP. Their SD-WAN members are the regional overlays only, and cross-region traffic uses the same members.
- Regional hubs are interconnected in a **full mesh**, with BGP sessions exchanging routes between regions.

## Routing specifics for multiregion topologies

Fortinet documents two inter-region routing designs. The choice turns on one requirement: **do you need ADVPN shortcuts across regions?**

| | eBGP between regions, intra-region ADVPN | iBGP between regions, inter-region ADVPN |
|---|---|---|
| AS plan | One AS per region | One AS across all regions |
| What crosses between regions | Each regional hub advertises a **summary** of its region to the other regions | Specific prefixes, **with their original BGP next hops preserved**. Each regional hub reflects them to its branches and to the remote hubs |
| What branches learn | Default or summary routes from their regional hub | Every prefix of every site |
| Spoke-to-spoke across regions | Always through both regional hubs | Direct cross-regional shortcuts that bypass both hubs, plus spoke-to-remote-hub shortcuts |
| ADVPN scope | Within each region only | Within and across regions |
| Fortinet's preference | **Preferred when cross-region ADVPN is not required** | Required when cross-region ADVPN is needed |

Cross-regional shortcuts need the shortcut messages to travel hub → hub → spoke. The phase 1 option for that is `auto-discovery-forwarder enable`, which the CLI Reference describes as enabling the forwarding of auto-discovery shortcut messages. It goes on the inter-hub overlays, while `auto-discovery-sender` stays on the hub's spoke-facing dial-up phase 1s and `auto-discovery-receiver` on the spokes.

> **Exam tip:** A spoke configuration doesn't change between the two designs. What changes is the hub-to-hub routing (eBGP summaries vs iBGP with preserved next hops) and whether shortcut messages are forwarded between regions.

### Provisioning regions

Fortinet's comparison of provisioning tools: the **Fabric Overlay Orchestrator** (FortiOS, single hub) doesn't do multiregion. The **FortiManager overlay template** does, but with additional manual steps. **Overlay-as-a-Service** doesn't. For large multiregion estates, the architecture guide points to the FortiManager SD-WAN Overlay Orchestrator with its IPsec and BGP templates.

## Use cases: multi-PoP, multi-hub failover

A region can contain several **PoPs**, each with several hubs. Since FortiOS 7.4.1, SD-WAN can fail traffic between PoPs in both directions:

| Piece | Setting | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Zone per PoP on the spoke | `config zone` → `edit "PoP1"` → `set minimum-sla-meet-members 2` | The zone is in SLA only while at least two of its members are. Otherwise the whole zone counts as out of SLA |
| Rule steers by zone | `config service` → `set mode sla`, `set zone-mode enable`, `config sla` → `set priority-zone "PoP1" "PoP2"` | The first zone listed is primary |
| Failback control | `set hold-down-time 30`, `set sla-stickiness enable` | After PoP1 recovers, existing sessions stay where they are while in SLA. New sessions move back after the hold-down |
| Inbound path | SD-WAN neighbor with `set service-id 1` + BGP `route-map-out-preferable` (in-SLA community) / `route-map-out` (out-of-SLA community) | Hubs translate the community into a short or long AS path toward external peers, so inbound traffic follows the outbound PoP |
| Hub health toward the core | Hub SD-WAN neighbor naming the spokes' **neighbor group**, with a health check to external peers | If a hub loses its external paths, it denies external routes to the spokes, which then use another hub |

The last row matters for scale: spokes measure only overlay quality to each hub, hubs measure the services beyond them, and BGP carries the result. That keeps health-check probe counts manageable in large deployments.

## Common MSSP deployments with SD-WAN

A managed service provider runs SD-WAN for many customers. Fortinet's MSSP deployment guide pushes a disciplined workflow: **model devices** and **device blueprints** (ZTP or low-touch), template groups with Jinja-driven provisioning templates, policy packages, and automation through the FortiManager JSON API. It discourages Device Discovery onboarding and per-device configuration in Device Manager, because neither can be tracked or replicated at scale.

The tenancy designs MSSPs typically choose between:

| Design | Isolation | Cost / scale | Fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dedicated hubs per customer (own FortiGates or VMs) | Complete | Highest hardware/licensing cost | Large or regulated customers |
| Shared hub FortiGate with a **VDOM per customer** | Separate configuration, policies, routing tables and admins per VDOM | VDOM count and licences per hub | Customers that need their own policy administration |
| **Segmentation over a single overlay** (VRFs) | Separate routing tables (VRFs) inside one VDOM; one set of tunnels carries all segments | Fewer tunnels, BGP sessions and health checks | Many small tenants or departments that need routing isolation but share infrastructure, including overlapping IP space |

On the FortiManager side, ADOMs separate customers administratively, while metadata variables and device blueprints keep each customer's branches identical.

## VRF-aware overlays (segmentation over a single overlay)

The goal: branches host several companies or departments, each in its own VRF. Same-VRF traffic may cross the SD-WAN, *including over ADVPN shortcuts*, but VRFs never mix, and they can reuse the same subnets.

### The pieces

1. **Encapsulation.** On every overlay phase 1, hub and spokes: `set encapsulation vpn-id-ipip`. FortiOS carries the VRF identity inside the tunnel (VPN ID with IPIP encapsulation). It applies to forwarded traffic only, not local-out traffic.
2. **PE and CE VRFs.** The tunnels (VRF-aware overlays) live in the **PE VRF**. Each LAN interface is assigned to a **CE VRF** (`set vrf <id>` on the interface). A typical node has one PE VRF and several CE VRFs. VRF 0 is a special case.
3. **BGP VPNv4.** Routes are exchanged as VPNv4 with route distinguishers and route targets:

```text
config router bgp
    set ibgp-multipath enable
    set additional-path-vpnv4 enable
    config vrf
        edit "0"
            set role pe
        next
        edit "1"
            set role ce
            set rd "1:1"
            set export-rt "1:1"
            set import-rt "1:1"
        next
        edit "2"
            set role ce
            set rd "2:1"
            set export-rt "2:1"
            set import-rt "2:1"
        next
    end
    config neighbor
        edit "172.16.255.252"
            set soft-reconfiguration-vpnv4 enable
            set additional-path-vpnv4 both
            ...
        next
    end
end
```

`role` is `standalone`, `ce` or `pe`. The `rd`, `export-rt`, `import-rt` and `import-route-map` options exist only for CE VRFs. `config leak-target` (formerly `config target` under `vrf-leak` in 7.0) leaks routes between VRFs, for example to give a CE VRF internet access through the PE or underlay VRF.

4. **SD-WAN, VRF-aware.** Health checks can set `set vrf <id>` with a `source`, so the spoke tags probes into the right VRF and the hub answers from a server in that VRF. Overlay stickiness keeps hub transit traffic on the overlay it arrived on: zone `set service-sla-tie-break input-device`, and on the rule `set input-zone` + `set tie-break input-device`.

### Limits and FortiManager

- Fortinet's 7.6 architecture guide gives **512 VRFs per VDOM (0–511) from FortiOS 7.6.1**: one PE VRF plus up to 510 CE VRFs, with VRF 0 set aside. The admin guide's segmentation page still quotes an older figure of 252, so check the release you deploy.
- In the FortiManager overlay template, **Segmentation Over Single Overlay** configures every tunnel with `vpn-id-ipip` and generates the VPNv4, VRF and PE/CE BGP configuration.
- Internet access per CE VRF needs inter-VRF leaking and NAT. Fortinet's MSSP guide uses an inter-VRF link plus a loopback IP pool for remote internet access through the hub, because the inter-VRF address isn't reachable across the overlay.

> **Watch out:** `vpn-id-ipip` must match on both ends of every overlay. A hub tunnel without it cannot decode the VRF of incoming traffic, so forwarded VRF traffic fails. BGP between the loopbacks can still come up, because the encapsulation applies only to forwarded traffic, not to local-out traffic.

## Lab it yourself

Multiregion, multi-PoP and MSSP designs need many FortiGates (at least two regional hubs and a spoke per region), far beyond the FortiGate-VM trial's one VM per FortiCloud account, 3 interfaces, 3 policies and 3 routes. Use the paid on-demand labs or licensed VMs. The trial's two VDOMs (root must stay the admin type) let you try one traffic VDOM. On a single trial you can also create two VRFs on LAN interfaces and inspect `get router info routing-table all` per VRF. A VRF-aware overlay needs a second FortiGate and VPNv4 BGP, so model it in the FortiManager trial (Segmentation Over Single Overlay) and preview the generated CLI.

## Key takeaways

- Multiregion: spokes attach only to regional hubs, and regional hubs form a full mesh with BGP between them.
- eBGP between regions (summaries, intra-region ADVPN only) is preferred unless cross-region ADVPN is required. Then use iBGP with original next hops preserved.
- `auto-discovery-forwarder` forwards shortcut messages and belongs on the inter-hub overlays for cross-regional shortcuts.
- Multi-PoP failover: zone `minimum-sla-meet-members`, rule `zone-mode` + `priority-zone`, `hold-down-time`, `sla-stickiness`, SD-WAN neighbor `service-id`.
- MSSP: model devices, blueprints, templates and API, with no per-device configuration. Tenancy by dedicated hub, VDOM or VRF segmentation.
- VRF-aware overlays: `encapsulation vpn-id-ipip`, tunnels in the PE VRF, LANs in CE VRFs, BGP VPNv4 with RD/RT, VRF-aware health checks.
