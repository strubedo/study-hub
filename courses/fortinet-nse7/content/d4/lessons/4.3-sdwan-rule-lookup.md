## SD-WAN rules are policy routes

An SD-WAN rule (CLI: a **service** under `config system sdwan` → `config service`) is a policy route with extra intelligence. Each enabled rule is installed in the FortiGate's policy-route table, which is why it appears in `diagnose firewall proute list` with a `vwl_service=<id>(<name>)` field next to your own `config router policy` entries.

That one fact explains most of the lookup behaviour:

1. The FortiGate evaluates **policy routes first**, in this precedence: regular policy routes (`config router policy`) > ISDB static routes (`config router static` with `internet-service`) > **SD-WAN rules**.
2. Within the SD-WAN rules, evaluation is **top-down, first match**, in the order shown in **Network > SD-WAN > SD-WAN Rules** (reorder with drag-and-drop or `move` in the CLI). Rule IDs range from 1 to 4000 but the ID is not the order.
3. If no policy route or SD-WAN rule matches, the FortiGate falls back to the **routing table (FIB)**. For traffic toward an SD-WAN zone this is the **implicit rule**: ECMP over the members that have equal routes, using `load-balance-mode`.
4. No route at all: the packet is dropped.

Route lookup normally happens for the first packet in each direction of a session and the result is cached in the session (the session lesson in 4.4 covers what makes it repeat).

## What "matching" means

A rule matches a packet only when **both** of these are true:

- The packet fits the rule's **traffic criteria**: source (address, `input-device`, users/groups), destination (address, protocol/port, internet service, application, route tag, TOS/DSCP).
- At least one of the rule's members (or zones) can actually forward the packet. Before an SD-WAN policy route is used, FortiOS checks the FIB:
  - the FIB **best match** for the destination must be an SD-WAN member, and
  - a FIB route to the destination must exist **through the member** the rule wants to use.

If the strategy can't produce a usable member (all members dead, out of the allowed set, or without a route to the destination), the rule doesn't apply to that packet and the next rule is evaluated, finally ending at the routing table. The CLI-only `set gateway enable` and `set default enable` options change those FIB checks (lesson 4.4).

> **Exam tip:** The most common SD-WAN rule "bug" is a missing route. If the overlay member `HUB1-VPN2` has no route to 10.0.1.0/24, a rule that prefers `HUB1-VPN2` for that destination can't use it, no matter what the health check says. Check `get router info routing-table all` before you blame the strategy.

## The implicit rule

The last line in the SD-WAN Rules table, called **sd-wan** in the GUI, can't be deleted or moved. It is not a policy route; it simply means "use the routing table". With a default route through the SD-WAN zone, each alive member's gateway is an equal-cost next hop, and `config system sdwan` → `set load-balance-mode` picks among them:

| `load-balance-mode` | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `source-ip-based` (default) | Same source IP, same member |
| `source-dest-ip-based` | Same source/destination pair, same member |
| `weight-based` | Session share by member `weight` (1-255) |
| `usage-based` | Spillover: first member until its `spillover-threshold` / `ingress-spillover-threshold`, then the next |
| `measured-volume-based` | Volume share by member `volume-ratio` (SD-WAN only) |

Members can't be excluded from implicit-rule ECMP with a weight of 0; a member whose static route has a worse **priority** is left out of ECMP. With dynamic routes, sessions are balanced equally regardless of weight.

## Designing the rule table

Order rules from **most specific to least specific**, exactly like firewall policies:

| Order | Rule | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Critical voice to the DC, best quality on jitter | Specific source/destination/DSCP; must not be caught by a broader rule |
| 2 | Corporate traffic to `CORP_LAN`, lowest cost (SLA) over overlays | Broad internal destination |
| 3 | SaaS by internet service (Microsoft-365, Salesforce) over local DIA | ISDB destinations |
| 4 | Guest traffic, manual, `wan2` only | Source-based |
| last | implicit rule | Everything else, ECMP by `load-balance-mode` |

Two placement traps:

- A broad rule (destination `all`, or a big supernet) above a specific one shadows it permanently.
- Application-based rules can't match the **first** session of an application (the application isn't identified yet), so a broad rule below them catches those first sessions. Design the fallback rule to be acceptable for unidentified traffic (lesson on matching criteria).

## SD-WAN for local-out traffic

Traffic the FortiGate itself originates (FortiGuard, DNS, FortiAnalyzer logs, RADIUS/LDAP, NTP, syslog, central management, SNMP traps) is **local-out** traffic. By default it uses a plain **routing table** lookup and ignores SD-WAN rules. Many services let you change that:

```text
config log fortianalyzer setting
    set interface-select-method sdwan
end
config system central-management
    set interface-select-method specify
    set interface "port1"
end
```

- `interface-select-method auto` (default): routing table.
- `sdwan`: SD-WAN rules or policy routes are applied to the local-out traffic.
- `specify`: a fixed interface (with an optional `source-ip`).
- **Network > Local Out Routing** shows all of these in one GUI page once enabled in **Feature Visibility**.
- For tests, `execute ping-options use-sdwan yes` and `execute traceroute-options use-sdwan yes` make diagnostic packets follow SD-WAN rules.

> **Watch out:** SD-WAN health-check probes are not local-out traffic that follows SD-WAN rules. Each health check is sent out of each of its members directly, using dedicated kernel routes (next lesson), and the member's `source` setting selects the source IP.

## Monitoring rule status

| Where | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Network > SD-WAN > SD-WAN Rules** | Each rule with its members, the currently selected member highlighted, and **hit count** |
| `diagnose sys sdwan service4 [id]` | Per rule: mode, flags, link-cost factor, health check, the **ordered member list** (first `selected` member wins), each member's state (`alive`/`dead`), SLA bitmap `sla(0x1)`, cost, and the match criteria |
| `diagnose firewall proute list` | The installed policy route for each rule (`vwl_service=1(name)`), its `oif=` list in order, match fields and `hit_count` |
| `diagnose sys sdwan health-check [name]` | Per member: `state(alive/dead)`, packet loss, latency, jitter, `sla_map` |
| `diagnose sys sdwan member` | Member interface, gateway, priority, weight |
| `diagnose sys sdwan internet-service-app-ctrl-list` | Learned application 3-tuples for application-based rules |
| `diagnose sys session list` | `sdwan_mbr_seq=` and `sdwan_service_id=` of each session |

A typical `service4` entry in 7.6:

```text
FGT-Spoke1 # diagnose sys sdwan service4 1

Service(1): Address Mode(IPV4) flags=0x200 use-shortcut-sla
 Tie break: cfg
  Gen(4), TOS(0x0/0x0), Protocol(0): src(1->65535):dst(1->65535), Mode(sla), sla-compare-order
  Members(2):
    1: Seq_num(3 HUB1-VPN1 HUB1), alive, sla(0x1), gid(0), cfg_order(0), local cost(0), selected
    2: Seq_num(4 HUB1-VPN2 HUB1), alive, sla(0x1), gid(0), cfg_order(1), local cost(0), selected
  Src address(1):
        10.1.1.0-10.1.1.255

  Dst address(1):
        10.0.0.0-10.255.255.255
```

Members are listed in preference order; both are `selected` (usable), and the first one (`HUB1-VPN1`) is the one new sessions use. A dead member loses `selected`; a member that fails the SLA shows `sla(0x0)` and drops below the members that meet it.

## Lab it yourself

The **FortiGate-VM trial** is enough to study rule lookup with two members: port1 and port2 as SD-WAN members toward two gateways (a Linux router with two interfaces works), port3 as LAN. That is the full 3-interface budget, so manage the VM through port3. One LAN-to-SD-WAN policy, one default route through the zone and two SD-WAN rules stay inside the limits (SD-WAN rules aren't static routes). Watch `diagnose firewall proute list` and `diagnose sys sdwan service4` while you reorder rules, and remove the route through one member to see the rule stop using it.

## Key takeaways

- SD-WAN rules are policy routes: precedence is policy routes > ISDB routes > SD-WAN rules, then the routing table.
- SD-WAN rules are evaluated top-down, first match; the implicit rule (routing table + `load-balance-mode`) is always last.
- A rule is used only if a chosen member passes the FIB checks: best match is an SD-WAN member and a route to the destination exists through that member.
- Default implicit-rule algorithm is **source-ip-based**.
- Local-out traffic uses the routing table unless the service sets `interface-select-method sdwan` (or `specify`).
- `diagnose sys sdwan service4` shows the ordered, selected members per rule; `diagnose firewall proute list` shows the installed policy routes.
