## Panorama log collection deployments

Panorama can receive firewall logs in two ways:

| Deployment | What it is |
|------------|-----------|
| **Local log collector** | The Panorama management server stores logs itself (Panorama mode on an M-Series or Panorama virtual appliance with log disks) |
| **Dedicated Log Collector** | A separate appliance (M-Series or Panorama virtual appliance) in **Log Collector mode**: it ingests and stores logs but has no web UI of its own for policy; it is managed from Panorama |

A Panorama in **Panorama mode** has a built-in **default** collector group containing its local log collector. A Dedicated Log Collector is added as a **managed collector** (**Panorama > Managed Collectors**) and then placed in a **collector group**.

> **Exam tip:** Management and logging can be split: the Panorama management server handles configuration; Dedicated Log Collectors scale log ingestion and storage separately. Panorama also queries the collectors on demand to show **Monitor** pages, reports and ACC (the logs stay on the collectors, they are not copied to Panorama).

## Managed collectors

**Panorama > Managed Collectors > Add** and enter:

* **Serial number** of the Dedicated Log Collector (or of a Panorama in Panorama mode)
* Management IP, hostname and (as needed) HA peer for the collector's own management network
* **Disks**: add log disks (disk pairs) and apply RAID in the collector's configuration
* **Communication**: collectors and Panorama authenticate with each other (a pre-shared key or custom certificates for secure server communication under **Panorama > Managed Collectors > Communication**)

After adding a managed collector, you **commit to Panorama**, then **Commit and push to Collector Groups** (a separate commit type) to push the collector configuration.

```text
Panorama-HQ> show log-collector all
Panorama-HQ> show log-collector serial-number <log-collector_SN>
LC1> show log-collector detail
```

`show log-collector all` on Panorama (or `show log-collector detail` on a Dedicated Log Collector) lists each collector's connection, commit and redistribution status and its **SearchEngine status**, which should be **Active** before you continue with replacement or upgrade work. The output layout varies by release.

## Collector groups

A **collector group** (**Panorama > Collector Groups**) is a set of Log Collectors that act as one logical log store. Firewalls are assigned to a group, and every log from a firewall goes into one of the group's collectors.

Collector group settings:

| Tab | Purpose |
|-----|---------|
| **General** | Name, **Log Storage** quota per log type (traffic, threat, url, etc.), **Min Retention Period** (days), and **Enable log redundancy across collectors** |
| **Monitoring** | SNMP and other monitoring of collectors |
| **Device Log Forwarding** | The **log forwarding preference list**: which collectors a given set of firewalls forward to, in order |
| **Collector Log Forwarding** | Forward logs from the collector group on to syslog, email or SNMP targets (this is the "forward from Panorama" path) |
| **Log Ingestion** | (Optional) allow the collector to ingest logs from external sources such as syslog sources, where configured |

Rules for a collector group:

* Collectors in a group must be the **same Panorama model**.
* Logs are **distributed across the collectors** in the group; the **preference list** decides which collectors a firewall sends to and the order for failover. The preference list does not strictly pin where every log lands; with multiple collectors the group balances storage.
* The group must have **disks** in each collector (log disk pairs) before it can store logs.

### Preference lists

On **Device Log Forwarding** you add a firewall (or a Panorama device group of firewalls) and choose **Collector Group Members** in priority order. A firewall sends its logs to the first available collector in its list; if that collector is unavailable, it uses the next. A balanced design sets half the firewalls to prefer LC1 then LC2 and the other half LC2 then LC1.

### Redundancy

With **Enable log redundancy across collectors**, each log is written to **two** different collectors in the group. That gives resilience if a collector fails, at the cost of **half the effective storage** and a lower maximum ingestion rate. Redundancy needs every collector in the group to have the **same number of log disks**.

Design points:

* A group with redundancy should have **at least 3 collectors** in current releases; Palo Alto's quorum rules apply, and a two-collector group becomes non-operational if one collector is down, so it does not give true high availability.
* If syslog forwarding is configured on the collector group, redundancy sends **both copies** of each log, producing duplicate syslog messages. Deduplicate at the SIEM or disable redundancy if syslog is the priority.
* **Panorama HA** is separate from collector redundancy: Panorama's two peers share config; log redundancy is a collector-group feature.

> **Watch out:** A firewall that can reach Panorama but is not assigned to any collector group and has no preference list entry keeps its logs **locally** and Panorama shows no logs for it. Panorama still lists it as connected, because the configuration channel and the log channel are different.

## Getting a firewall to forward

1. **Panorama-side**: add the firewall to a collector group through the preference list (or enable a template/device group setting that sets log forwarding).
2. **Firewall-side**: the firewall learns its collector list when Panorama pushes (Commit > Push to Devices). Firewalls must be managed by Panorama with a successful connection.
3. **Rules**: a log forwarding profile with Forward Method *Panorama/Logging Service*.
4. **Connectivity**: firewalls send logs to collectors over TCP 3978, and Panorama communicates with collectors over the management network; allow these through any intermediate filter.

```text
FW-A> show log-collector preference-list
FW-A> show logging-status
Panorama-HQ> debug log-receiver statistics
```

On the firewall, `show log-collector preference-list` shows which collectors it forwards to, and `show logging-status` reports whether the log forwarding agent (Panorama, the Log Collector, or the Strata Logging Service) is active and connected.

## Log Collector upgrades and compatibility

* Panorama must run a version **equal to or later than** the Log Collectors and managed firewalls.
* Upgrade order for a Panorama estate: **Panorama first**, then **Dedicated Log Collectors**, then the firewalls. Details are in lesson 2.4.

## Local logs and the log quota

* On the firewall, **Device > Setup > Management > Logging and Reporting Settings** sets per-log-type **quotas** on the local disk and the pre-defined reports.
* On Panorama and collectors, quotas and retention are set per collector group.

## Lab it yourself

You can run a Panorama VM and a second Panorama VM in Log Collector mode (trial/eval licence required; check availability).

1. Deploy a Panorama VM in Panorama mode. Add a firewall as a managed device.
2. Deploy a second Panorama VM in Log Collector mode, add it under Managed Collectors, add a log disk, create a collector group `CG-HQ`, put the collector in it and commit to the collector group.
3. In the group's Device Log Forwarding tab, assign the firewall to the collector.
4. Attach a log forwarding profile to a rule, generate traffic, and see the logs under Panorama Monitor > Logs.
5. Disable the collector and observe where the firewall stores logs and what the Panorama views show.

## Key takeaways

* Panorama can store logs locally or on Dedicated Log Collectors that act as managed collectors.
* A collector group is the unit that stores logs; firewalls reach it through a log forwarding preference list.
* Log redundancy writes each log to two collectors and halves effective capacity; use three or more collectors.
* Collectors in a group are the same model; redundancy needs identical disk counts.
* Collector Log Forwarding sends logs from the group to syslog, email or SNMP.
* A firewall can be connected to Panorama and still have no log path; configuration and logging are separate.
* Upgrade Panorama first, then collectors, then firewalls.
