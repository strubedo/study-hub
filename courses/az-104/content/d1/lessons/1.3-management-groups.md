## Why management groups

With one subscription you assign roles and policies on the subscription. With twenty, repeating every assignment twenty times is error-prone. **Management groups** are containers **above subscriptions**: assign a policy or role once to a management group and every subscription, resource group and resource beneath it inherits it.

```text
Tenant Root Group  (ID = tenant ID)
├── mg-contoso-platform
│   ├── Contoso-Identity        (subscription)
│   └── Contoso-Connectivity    (subscription)
├── mg-contoso-landingzones
│   ├── mg-contoso-prod
│   │   └── Contoso-Prod        (subscription)
│   └── mg-contoso-nonprod
│       └── Contoso-Dev         (subscription)
└── mg-contoso-sandbox
```

## Rules and limits

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Management groups per directory (tenant) | 10,000 |
| Depth of the tree | Up to **six levels**, **not counting** the root level or the subscription level |
| Parents | Each management group and each subscription has **exactly one parent** |
| Children | A management group can have many children (management groups and subscriptions) |
| Tags | Management groups **cannot be tagged** |
| Tenancy | A hierarchy lives in one tenant; subscriptions from another tenant cannot be added |

## The root management group

- Every tenant has one top-level **root management group**, display name **Tenant Root Group** by default, whose **ID equals the Microsoft Entra tenant ID**.
- It **cannot be moved or deleted**.
- **New subscriptions are placed under the root** by default, unless you set a different **default management group** in **Hierarchy settings**. Setting a default (for example `mg-contoso-sandbox`) means new subscriptions land where your guardrail policies apply.
- **Hierarchy settings** can also **require write permissions to create new management groups**, so not every user can create them (by default, any user in the tenant can create new management groups).
- **No one has default access** to the root management group. A Global Administrator must **elevate access** (User Access Administrator at `/`) and can then assign roles on the root. Anything assigned at the root applies to **every** subscription in the tenant, so keep root-level assignments to the minimum.
- Renaming the root's display name needs Owner or Contributor on it.

## Inheritance

Role assignments and policy assignments on a management group flow down to everything below, exactly like a subscription's assignments flow to its resource groups:

- **Reader** for the security team on `mg-contoso-landingzones` gives read access to Contoso-Prod and Contoso-Dev and every future subscription placed under it.
- **Virtual Machine Contributor** on a management group has no meaning for the group itself but inherits down to every VM beneath it.
- A **deny** policy such as "Allowed locations" on `mg-contoso-prod` applies to every subscription under it; you cannot override it lower down, only exclude scopes in the assignment or create exemptions.
- A **custom role** can name one management group in its `AssignableScopes` and is then assignable at that group and anywhere below. Custom roles with `DataActions` cannot be assigned at management group scope.
- Policy assignments at a management group evaluate resources in the subscriptions and resource groups below; the management group itself has no resources.

Remember the **500 role assignments per management group** limit; subscriptions have a separate 5,000.

## Permissions on management groups

| Role | Create | Rename | Move | Delete | Assign access | Assign policy | Read |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Owner | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Contributor | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | | | Yes |
| Management Group Contributor | Yes | Yes | Yes (see below) | Yes | | | Yes |
| Reader | | | | | | | Yes |
| Management Group Reader | | | | | | | Yes |
| Resource Policy Contributor | | | | | | Yes | |
| User Access Administrator | | | | | Yes | Yes | |

This follows Microsoft's table, which leaves **Read** empty for User Access Administrator even though the role definition includes `*/read`; don't read much into that cell. Management Group Contributor and Management Group Reader act only on the management group scope itself.

### Moving a subscription or management group

To move a **subscription** to a different management group you need:

- On the **subscription** (the child being moved): permissions that include `Microsoft.Authorization/roleAssignments/write` and `/delete` and `Microsoft.Management/managementGroups/subscriptions/write`; in practice **Owner** on the subscription.
- On the **target parent** management group: `Microsoft.Management/managementGroups/write` (Owner, Contributor or Management Group Contributor).
- On the **current parent** management group: the same write permission.

Moving a management group needs the same on the management group being moved and on both parents. **Exception:** when the current or target parent is the **root** management group, no permission on the root is needed. If your Owner role on the subscription is only *inherited* from the current management group, you can move the subscription only to a management group where you are also Owner. When a subscription moves, it immediately **stops inheriting** the old parent's roles and policies and **starts inheriting** the new parent's.

> **Exam tip:** "User1 is Owner of the Contoso-Dev subscription and Management Group Contributor on mg-nonprod, but cannot move Contoso-Dev from mg-prod to mg-nonprod." Check the **current** parent: User1 also needs write on **mg-prod**.

## Creating and managing

Portal: search **Management groups**, then **Create**, enter **Management group ID** (cannot be changed later) and **display name**. Open a group and select **Add subscription** or **Move** to change a parent. The **Hierarchy settings** page holds the default management group and the creation permission setting.

Azure CLI:

```bash
az account management-group create --name mg-contoso-prod --display-name "Contoso Production"
az account management-group create --name mg-contoso-dev --display-name "Contoso Dev" --parent mg-contoso-landingzones
az account management-group subscription add --name mg-contoso-prod --subscription 00000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000000
az account management-group update --name mg-contoso-dev --parent mg-contoso-nonprod
az account management-group show --name mg-contoso-prod --expand --recurse
az account management-group delete --name mg-contoso-dev
```

Azure PowerShell:

```powershell
New-AzManagementGroup -GroupName 'mg-contoso-prod' -DisplayName 'Contoso Production'
New-AzManagementGroupSubscription -GroupName 'mg-contoso-prod' -SubscriptionId '00000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000000'
$parent = Get-AzManagementGroup -GroupName 'mg-contoso-nonprod'
Update-AzManagementGroup -GroupName 'mg-contoso-dev' -ParentId $parent.Id
Get-AzManagementGroup -GroupName 'mg-contoso-prod' -Expand -Recurse
Remove-AzManagementGroup -GroupName 'mg-contoso-dev'
```

All these cmdlets take the management group **ID** in `-GroupName` (`-GroupId` is accepted as an alias), not the display name.

A management group can only be deleted when it has **no children** (no child management groups or subscriptions); move them out first.

Role assignment at management group scope uses the scope string `/providers/Microsoft.Management/managementGroups/<group-id>`:

```bash
az role assignment create --assignee-object-id <group-object-id> --assignee-principal-type Group \
  --role "Reader" --scope "/providers/Microsoft.Management/managementGroups/mg-contoso-prod"
```

And policy assignment: `az policy assignment create --scope "/providers/Microsoft.Management/managementGroups/mg-contoso-prod" --policy <definition> ...`.

## Design guidance

- Keep the hierarchy **shallow** (three or four levels is plenty) and based on **governance needs** (platform, production, non-production, sandbox), not the org chart, which changes.
- Assign broad guardrails (allowed regions, required tags, security baselines) high; assign workload-specific roles at subscription or resource group level.
- Use a **default management group** for new subscriptions so nothing lands unmanaged under the root.

## Try it yourself

Cost: nothing; management groups and assignments are free. In a personal tenant you may first need to open **Management groups** in the portal once so the root management group is created (it can take a few minutes), and you must have permission to create groups.

1. Cloud Shell: `az account management-group create --name mg-lab-parent --display-name "Lab Parent"` and `az account management-group create --name mg-lab-child --display-name "Lab Child" --parent mg-lab-parent`.
2. `az account management-group show --name mg-lab-parent --expand --recurse` and confirm the tree.
3. Assign **Reader** to a test group at `mg-lab-child` (scope string above). In the portal, open the management group's **Access control (IAM)** and find it.
4. Optional (only if you can move it back): move your subscription under `mg-lab-child`, open the subscription's IAM page and see the Reader assignment marked as inherited, then move the subscription back to its original parent.
5. Clean up: remove the role assignment, then `az account management-group delete --name mg-lab-child` and `az account management-group delete --name mg-lab-parent`. No resource group or billable resource is involved.

## Key takeaways

- Management groups sit above subscriptions; roles and policies assigned on them inherit to every subscription, resource group and resource below.
- Up to 10,000 per tenant, six levels deep (excluding root and subscription levels), one parent each; they cannot be tagged.
- The root management group (Tenant Root Group, ID = tenant ID) cannot be moved or deleted; new subscriptions land there unless a default management group is set.
- Nobody has default access to the root; a Global Administrator elevates access to manage it.
- Moving a subscription needs Owner on the subscription and write on both the old and the new parent management group.
- CLI `az account management-group ...`; PowerShell `New-AzManagementGroup`, `New-AzManagementGroupSubscription`, `Update-AzManagementGroup -ParentId`.
