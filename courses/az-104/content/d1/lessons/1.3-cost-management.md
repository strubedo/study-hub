## The tools

**Microsoft Cost Management** is built into the Azure portal (search **Cost Management**, or open **Cost Management** under any subscription or resource group). It works at **scopes**: management group, subscription and resource group (Azure RBAC scopes), plus billing scopes such as an EA enrollment or an MCA billing profile. The pieces AZ-104 tests:

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Cost analysis** | Explore actual and forecast costs: group by service, resource group, location or **tag**, filter, change the time range, save views, download |
| **Budgets** | A spending amount for a period, with **alert thresholds** that email people and can trigger **action groups** |
| **Cost alerts** | One place for budget alerts, credit alerts, department spending quota alerts and **anomaly alerts** |
| **Azure Advisor** (Cost category) | Recommendations such as shutting down or resizing under-used VMs and buying reservations or savings plans |
| **Exports** | Scheduled export of cost data to a storage account |

Cost and usage data typically arrives **8 to 24 hours** after use, so none of these are real-time. A brand-new subscription can take up to **48 hours** before all Cost Management features work.

### Who can do what

| Role | Cost data and budgets |
|---|---|
| **Owner**, **Contributor** | View costs; create, edit and delete budgets |
| **Cost Management Contributor** | View costs and manage cost configuration (budgets, exports) without any resource rights |
| **Cost Management Reader** | View cost data and budgets |
| **Reader** | View costs and budgets |
| **Billing Reader** | Read billing data |

For a finance analyst who must create budgets but not touch resources, **Cost Management Contributor** at the subscription is the least-privilege answer.

## Cost analysis

Open **Cost Management > Cost analysis** at a scope. Useful moves:

- **Group by** **Resource group name**, **Service name**, **Location** or a **Tag** (for example `CostCenter`) to build a chargeback view.
- Switch between **Actual cost** and **Amortized cost** (amortized spreads reservation purchases over their term).
- Look at **Forecast** in the accumulated cost chart.
- Save and share views; pin to a dashboard; **Download** to CSV or Excel.

> **Watch out:** Costs appear under a tag only for usage recorded **while the tag was applied to that resource**. Tags are not applied to historical data, and tags on a resource group are not inherited by its resources. Apply tags (or a modify policy that copies them) before the period you want to report on.

## Budgets

A budget is evaluated against **actual cost** every **24 hours**; when a threshold is crossed, emails are normally sent within an hour. Budgets **never stop resources or block spending** by themselves.

Configuration, in order:

1. **Scope**: subscription, resource group or management group (or a billing scope). Optional **filters** limit the budget to, for example, one resource group, a service such as Virtual Machines, or a tag value.
2. **Name** and **Reset period**: **Monthly**, **Quarterly** or **Annually** (pay-as-you-go and Visual Studio subscriptions can also choose **Billing month**, **Billing quarter**, **Billing year** to match the invoice period). The amount resets at the start of each period.
3. **Creation date** and **Expiration date**.
4. **Amount** (the portal suggests one based on forecast).
5. **Alert conditions**: each is a **Type** (**Actual** or **Forecasted**) and a **% of budget**. At least one threshold and one recipient email are required; the portal allows up to **five** thresholds and five emails. Thresholds can be from 0.01% to 1000%.
6. **Action groups** (only for budgets at **subscription** or **resource group** scope): call an Azure Monitor action group that can send SMS or push notifications, call a webhook, or run an **Azure Automation runbook**, **Logic App** or **Azure Function**. This is how you automatically shut down dev VMs when a budget is hit.

Example: budget of 1,000 USD per month on `rg-web-dev`, alerts at **Actual 80%** (email the team), **Actual 100%** (email plus action group that runs a runbook to deallocate VMs) and **Forecasted 110%** (early warning).

> **Exam tip:** "Automatically stop VMs when monthly spend in rg-dev reaches 500 USD" = a **budget** on the resource group with an **action group** that runs an **Automation runbook** (or Logic App / Function). A budget alone only notifies. "Get warned *before* the budget is exceeded" = a **Forecasted** alert condition.

PowerShell (Az.Billing):

```powershell
$ag = Get-AzActionGroup -ResourceGroupName 'rg-monitor' -Name 'ag-cost-ops'
New-AzConsumptionBudget -Name 'budget-web-dev' -Amount 1000 -Category Cost -TimeGrain Monthly `
  -StartDate 2026-12-01 -EndDate 2027-11-30 -ResourceGroupName 'rg-web-dev' `
  -ContactEmail 'ops@contoso.com' -NotificationKey 'actual80' -NotificationThreshold 80 `
  -NotificationEnabled -ContactGroup $ag.Id
```

`-NotificationThreshold` is a **percentage**: the cmdlet reference says it "is always percent and has to be between 0 and 1000", so `80` means 80% of the budget. (The budgets tutorial's PowerShell sample passes `0.8`, which by that definition is 0.8%; its CLI sample uses `80.0`.) The start date must be the first day of a month.

Azure CLI: the `az consumption budget` group is in **preview**. `az consumption budget create` creates a budget (amount, category, time grain, dates) but has no notification parameters; `az consumption budget create-with-rg` takes `--time-period` and `--notifications` JSON (threshold as a percentage, for example `80.0`) for resource group budgets. Many administrators create budgets in the portal or with ARM templates or Bicep (`Microsoft.Consumption/budgets`) instead.

```bash
az consumption budget create --budget-name budget-sub-monthly --category cost \
  --amount 2000 --time-grain monthly --start-date 2026-12-01 --end-date 2027-11-30
```

## Cost alerts

**Cost Management > Cost alerts** shows all active alerts for the scope:

- **Budget alerts**: thresholds you configured.
- **Credit alerts**: generated automatically at **90%** and **100%** of an EA **Azure Prepayment** balance.
- **Department spending quota alerts**: EA departments reaching a fixed share of their quota.
- **Anomaly alerts**: you subscribe a scope (subscription) to be emailed when Cost Management detects unusual spending.

## Azure Advisor cost recommendations

**Advisor > Cost** lists recommendations with estimated savings. The main ones:

- **Right-size or shut down under-utilized VMs and VM scale sets**, based on CPU, memory and network over a **lookback period** (default **7 days**; configurable to 7, 14, 21, 30, 60 or 90 days). Resize suggestions can cross VM series and include B-series burstable sizes.
- **Buy reservations** or **savings plans** for consistent usage.
- Delete idle or unattached resources (for example unattached disks or unused public IP addresses) and other service-specific savings.

Advisor savings estimates do not account for reservations or savings plans you already own, so the listed savings may be higher than you will actually see. Recommendations update after configuration changes, which can take up to 48 hours. You can **postpone** or **dismiss** a recommendation, and Advisor needs to write its results, so a **ReadOnly lock on the subscription** stops it working correctly.

## Reservations and savings plans (high level)

| | Azure Reservations | Azure savings plan for compute |
|---|---|---|
| Commitment | Specific resource type, size (or size family) and region | A fixed amount per hour |
| Term | One or three years | One or three years |
| Applies to | Matching usage only | Eligible compute usage across services and regions |
| Best for | Stable, predictable workloads | Changing or dynamic workloads |

Reservations usually give the deeper discount; savings plans give flexibility. Both are billing constructs: they do not create or change resources. Neither is an alerting tool.

## Other cost levers worth naming

- **Tags** plus **Cost analysis group by tag** for showback and chargeback; **modify** policies to make tagging reliable.
- **Azure Hybrid Benefit** for Windows Server and SQL Server licenses you already own.
- Deallocating (not just stopping inside the OS) dev VMs, auto-shutdown schedules, and choosing the right storage access tier.

## Try it yourself

Cost: nothing; budgets and Cost analysis are free.

1. Open your subscription > **Cost Management > Cost analysis**. Group by **Resource group name**, then by **Service name**. Switch to the **Forecast** view.
2. Create a resource group `rg-cost-lab` with a tag `CostCenter=LAB`.
3. **Budgets > Add**: scope `rg-cost-lab`, name `budget-lab`, monthly, amount 5 (in your billing currency). Alert conditions: **Actual 50%**, **Actual 100%**, **Forecasted 100%**, with your email. If you have an action group, attach it; otherwise note where it would go.
4. In Cloud Shell (PowerShell) run `Get-AzConsumptionBudget -ResourceGroupName rg-cost-lab` to see the budget.
5. Open **Advisor > Cost** and read any recommendations and the **Configuration** page lookback settings.
6. Clean up: delete the budget (`Remove-AzConsumptionBudget -Name budget-lab -ResourceGroupName rg-cost-lab`), then `az group delete -n rg-cost-lab --yes`.

## Key takeaways

- Cost Management works at management group, subscription and resource group scopes; data lags 8 to 24 hours.
- Budgets: monthly, quarterly or annual reset; Actual or Forecasted thresholds as % of budget; up to five thresholds and five emails in the portal; evaluated every 24 hours; never stop resources on their own.
- Action groups (subscription and resource group budgets) let a threshold run a runbook, Logic App or Function.
- Cost Management Contributor manages budgets without resource rights; Reader and Cost Management Reader view.
- Advisor cost: right-size or shut down VMs (7-day default lookback), buy reservations or savings plans, remove idle resources.
- Reservations commit to a resource and region; savings plans commit to an hourly spend; both one or three years.
- Tags only show in costs from the time they are on the resource; they are not inherited.
