## What a backup policy holds

A **backup policy** lives in a vault and defines **when** backups run (schedule, time zone) and **how long** each recovery point is kept (retention). One policy can protect many items; each item uses exactly one policy. A Recovery Services vault starts with built-in policies such as **DefaultPolicy** for VMs (one backup a day, recovery points kept 30 days); Learn notes the defaults can't be deleted permanently because Azure recreates them.

Policies are per workload type: an Azure VM policy can't be assigned to a file share.

## Azure VM policies: Standard and Enhanced

| | **Standard** | **Enhanced** |
|---|---|---|
| Schedule | **Daily** or **weekly**, once per day | **Hourly** (every 4, 6, 8, 12 or 24 hours over a window), daily or weekly |
| Instant restore snapshot retention | **1 to 5 days**, default **2**; fixed at 5 for weekly schedules | **1 to 30 days**, default **7** (the maximum is lower with frequent hourly schedules: Learn gives 17 days at every 4 hours, 22 at every 6) |
| Snapshot type | Incremental blob snapshots, even the first one (so no full point exists if the original disk is lost) | Managed disk snapshots; **first snapshot is a full copy**, then incremental |
| Zone resilience of snapshots | No | Snapshots can use ZRS |
| Newer offerings | Not for Premium SSD v2 or Ultra Disks; Trusted Launch only for newly protected VMs and only from the CLI (2.73.0+), Az PowerShell (14.0.0+) or REST API (2025-01-01+) | **Premium SSD v2, Ultra Disks**, Trusted Launch VMs, multidisk crash-consistent snapshots |
| Switch | Learn's migration article documents moving items from Standard to Enhanced without losing existing backups | A VM on Enhanced can't be moved back to Standard |

Backup runs in two phases: an **instant restore snapshot** is taken on the VM's disks (the "snapshot" or operational tier, kept for the instant restore retention), then the changes are transferred to the **vault** (the "vault-standard" tier, kept for the retention ranges). Restores from a snapshot are fast; restores from the vault take longer. Scheduled backups start **within two hours** of the scheduled time.

Learn's Enhanced policy page still carries older sentences ("Trusted Launch VMs only with Enhanced", "a VM on Standard can't start using Enhanced") next to newer ones. The dedicated migration article (updated June 2026) is the most specific source: Standard can protect an **unprotected** Trusted Launch VM only through the CLI 2.73.0+, Az PowerShell 14.0.0+ or REST API 2025-01-01+; to turn on Trusted Launch for a VM already protected by Standard, **migrate it to Enhanced first**; and migration is one-way. Exam questions about Trusted Launch, Premium SSD v2, Ultra Disks or "several backups a day" point to **Enhanced**.

## Retention ranges

A VM policy can keep points in up to four ranges:

| Range | Taken from | Maximum (Learn) |
|---|---|---|
| **Daily** | Every daily backup | 9,999 days (minimum 7) |
| **Weekly** | The backup on the chosen day(s) of the week | 5,163 weeks |
| **Monthly** | The backup on a chosen week and day ("first Sunday") or a chosen date ("day 1") | 1,188 months |
| **Yearly** | The backup in a chosen month on a chosen week and day or date | 99 years |

A recovery point can match several ranges (a first-Sunday backup is a daily, a weekly and a monthly point). Learn doesn't spell out the VM rule in one sentence; its Backup vault policy pages say retention rules are evaluated in priority order **yearly, monthly, weekly, daily** and the highest-priority matching rule applies. In a normal policy, where the longer ranges are the yearly and monthly ones, that means the point is kept for its longest range. Monthly retention counts every month as 31 days, so 12 months is 372 days.

The portal's new Enhanced policy suggests 180 days daily, 12 weeks, 60 months and 10 years; you can change them.

## Worked example

Policy **pol-vm-prod** (Standard, time zone UTC):

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Schedule | Daily at 23:00 |
| Instant restore | 2 days |
| Daily | 14 days |
| Weekly | Sunday, 8 weeks |
| Monthly | First Sunday, 12 months |
| Yearly | Not configured |

Today is **Monday 2026-11-23, 10:00 UTC**. Which of these recovery points still exist?

| Backup | Kept as | Expires | Exists today? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wed 2026-11-04 | Daily (14 days) | 2026-11-18 | **No** |
| Wed 2026-11-18 | Daily | 2026-12-02 | **Yes** |
| Sun 2026-09-20 | Weekly (8 weeks = 56 days) | 2026-11-15 | **No** |
| Sun 2026-10-11 | Weekly | 2026-12-06 | **Yes** |
| Sun 2026-10-04 | First Sunday of October: monthly | October 2027 | **Yes** |
| Sun 2026-11-01 | First Sunday of November: monthly | November 2027 | **Yes** |

The backups of 21 and 22 November also still have their **instant restore snapshots** (two days), so restoring from them is fastest.

> **Exam tip:** Draw the calendar. Count the retention from the backup date, then check whether the date is also a weekly, monthly or yearly point: in a normal policy the longest matching range decides.

## Changing a policy

Learn's FAQ is explicit:

- The new **schedule and retention** apply to every item that uses the policy from the next run.
- If retention is **extended**, existing recovery points are kept for the new, longer period.
- If retention is **reduced**, existing points that fall outside it are **marked for pruning** in the next cleanup job and deleted. With secure-by-default soft delete ("soft delete of recovery points"), points lost this way stay recoverable as **soft-deleted** for the vault's soft-delete period (not for SQL Server and SAP HANA log points), and an **immutable vault** blocks the reduction altogether. Pruning can take up to 24 hours to show.
- Retention is per range: adding a yearly range doesn't extend existing monthly points.
- You can't change the retention of one individual recovery point.

Assigning a **different policy** to an item has the same effect: the item's existing points take the new policy's retention.

## Azure Files policies

Azure Files backup is configured in a **Recovery Services vault** at the share level:

| Tier | How it works | Retention notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Snapshot** | Share snapshots kept in the storage account; Azure Backup takes a lease on them so they can't be deleted by accident | Up to **200** snapshots per share in total; daily points up to 200 days, weekly 200 weeks, monthly 120 months, yearly 10 years |
| **Vault-standard** (vaulted) | Snapshot first, then changed data copied to the vault, so a deleted storage account or ransomware doesn't take the backup with it | Snapshot up to 30 days, daily 9,999 days, weekly 5,163 weeks, monthly 1,188 months, yearly 99 years |

Schedules can be **daily** or **hourly** (multiple backups a day). Configuring backup also turns on share soft delete for the storage account.

## Blobs and disks (Backup vault)

**Azure Blobs** policies have two tiers, in one policy:

- **Operational backup**: continuous, local to the storage account. It sets blob **point-in-time restore** to the policy's retention (n days), **blob soft delete** to n + 5 days, enables **versioning** and **change feed**, and puts a **delete lock** on the account. Restores are point in time, for block blobs only.
- **Vaulted backup**: scheduled copies to the Backup vault using object replication, with daily, weekly, monthly and yearly retention. Learn's current policy experience always includes vaulted backup and doesn't create new operational-only policies.

**Azure Disks** policies support only the **operational tier**: incremental **snapshots** stored in a resource group you choose, on an **hourly** (1, 2, 4, 6, 8 or 12 hours) or **daily** schedule, crash-consistent and agentless. Retention is limited to one year or **450** snapshots per disk, whichever comes first (incremental snapshots are capped at 500 per disk, and the policy leaves headroom for on-demand backups). Premium SSD v2 and Ultra Disks support only the 12-hour hourly option or daily.

## Create a policy

**Portal:** vault > **Backup policies** > **+ Add** > choose the workload (Azure Virtual Machine, Azure Files, SQL Server in Azure VM, ...) > for VMs choose **Standard** or **Enhanced** > set schedule, instant restore, and retention ranges > **Create**. In a Backup vault: **Backup policies** > **+ Add** > datasource type (Azure Disks, Azure Blobs, ...) > schedule and retention rules.

**Azure CLI** (start from the default VM policy JSON, edit it, create a new policy):

```bash
az backup policy get-default-for-vm -g rg-bcdr-eus --vault-name rsv-contoso-eus > pol-vm-prod.json
# edit name, schedulePolicy.scheduleRunTimes, retentionPolicy.dailySchedule.retentionDuration.count, etc.
az backup policy create -g rg-bcdr-eus --vault-name rsv-contoso-eus --name pol-vm-prod \
  --backup-management-type AzureIaasVM --policy pol-vm-prod.json
az backup policy list -g rg-bcdr-eus --vault-name rsv-contoso-eus -o table
```

**Azure PowerShell:**

```powershell
$v = Get-AzRecoveryServicesVault -ResourceGroupName rg-bcdr-eus -Name rsv-contoso-eus
$sch = Get-AzRecoveryServicesBackupSchedulePolicyObject -WorkloadType AzureVM
$sch.ScheduleRunTimes.Clear()
$sch.ScheduleRunTimes.Add([datetime]::SpecifyKind((Get-Date "2026-11-23 23:00"), 'Utc'))
$ret = Get-AzRecoveryServicesBackupRetentionPolicyObject -WorkloadType AzureVM
$ret.DailySchedule.DurationCountInDays = 14
New-AzRecoveryServicesBackupProtectionPolicy -Name pol-vm-prod -WorkloadType AzureVM `
  -RetentionPolicy $ret -SchedulePolicy $sch -VaultId $v.ID
```

For an Enhanced hourly schedule, Learn's example uses `Get-AzRecoveryServicesBackupSchedulePolicyObject -WorkloadType AzureVM -BackupManagementType AzureVM -PolicySubType Enhanced -ScheduleRunFrequency Hourly`.

## Try it yourself

Cloud Shell. Cost: nothing (policies in an empty vault).

```bash
rg=rg-lab-d5pol; loc=eastus
az group create -n $rg -l $loc
az backup vault create -g $rg -n rsv-lab-pol -l $loc
az backup policy list -g $rg --vault-name rsv-lab-pol --query "[].{name:name, type:properties.backupManagementType}" -o table
az backup policy show -g $rg --vault-name rsv-lab-pol --name DefaultPolicy -o jsonc
```

1. In the portal, open the vault > **Backup policies** > **+ Add** > **Azure Virtual Machine**. Build the worked-example policy above, then switch the policy type to **Enhanced** and note the extra **Hourly** schedule and the 30-day instant restore range.
2. Create an Azure Files policy with an hourly schedule and look at how the maximum daily retention changes with the number of backups per day.
3. Work out on paper which of your policy's recovery points would exist on 1 January 2027.

Clean up: `az group delete -n $rg --yes --no-wait` (nothing is protected, so the vault deletes normally).

## Key takeaways

- A policy = schedule + retention; one per item; DefaultPolicy is daily with 30-day retention.
- Standard: daily or weekly, instant restore 1–5 days (default 2). Enhanced: hourly every 4 to 24 hours, instant restore 1–30 days (default 7), Premium SSD v2, Ultra and Trusted Launch support; Standard items can migrate to Enhanced, never back.
- Retention ranges: daily (min 7, max 9,999 days), weekly, monthly, yearly (99 years); a point that matches several ranges takes the highest-priority one (yearly > monthly > weekly > daily), normally its longest.
- Extending retention extends existing points; reducing it prunes them (soft delete keeps pruned points recoverable; immutable vaults block the change).
- Azure Files: snapshot tier (200 snapshots) or vaulted tier; blobs: operational (point-in-time restore, soft delete n + 5) and vaulted; disks: operational snapshots only.
