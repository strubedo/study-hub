## Paying for what you access

Block blob storage is priced on two axes: **capacity** (GB per month) and **access** (transactions and data retrieval). **Access tiers** let you trade one for the other. Cooler tiers cost less to store and more to read, and they have a **minimum retention period**: remove or move a blob earlier and you pay an **early deletion** charge for the remaining days.

Tiers apply to **block blobs** (including versions and snapshots) in **general-purpose v2** and legacy Blob Storage accounts. Append blobs and page blobs can't be tiered, and blobs in a **premium block blob** account have no hot/cool/cold/archive tiers.

## The tiers

| Tier | Online? | Minimum retention (GPv2) | Storage cost | Access cost | Typical data |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hot** | Yes | None | Highest | Lowest | Active data, frequently read or written |
| **Cool** | Yes | **30 days** | Lower | Higher | Short-term backup, older media still served occasionally |
| **Cold** | Yes | **90 days** | Lower still | Higher still | Rarely read data that must still be readable instantly |
| **Archive** | **No (offline)** | **180 days** | Lowest | Highest; retrieval takes hours | Compliance archives, long-term backups |

- Online tiers (hot, cool, cold) have the same latency and throughput; only availability (slightly lower SLA for cool and cold) and price differ.
- In legacy **Blob Storage** accounts, cool and cold have no minimum retention; the numbers above are for GPv2.
- Microsoft Learn also describes a newer **smart tier** option that moves data among hot, cool and cold automatically based on usage. It is not part of the 2023 objective; check the current article before relying on it.

### Early deletion, worked

The charge is prorated for the days remaining. Microsoft's example: a blob moved to archive and deleted or moved to hot after **45 days** is charged for **135 more days** (180 − 45) of archive storage. A blob placed in cool and deleted after 10 days is charged for 20 more days of cool storage.

Things that count as removal: **delete**, **overwrite** (Put Blob, Put Block List, Copy Blob onto it) and **changing tier** (to any tier, warmer or cooler). In an account with blob soft delete enabled, a soft-deleted blob is not yet "deleted" for this purpose until its retention expires.

## The account default tier

Every GPv2 account has a **default access tier**: **Hot** (the default) or **Cool** or **Cold**. **Archive can never be the default**. New blobs uploaded without an explicit tier get the default and are shown as **inferred**. Changing the account default re-tiers every blob whose tier was inferred, which can incur write charges (to a cooler tier) or read and retrieval charges (to a warmer tier).

```bash
az storage account update -g rg-data-prod -n stcontosodata01 --access-tier Cool
```

```powershell
Set-AzStorageAccount -ResourceGroupName rg-data-prod -Name stcontosodata01 -AccessTier Cool
```

## Changing a blob's tier

Three methods:

1. **Set Blob Tier** on an existing blob (portal **Change tier**, CLI `az storage blob set-tier`, PowerShell `SetAccessTier`). Best for moving **cooler**. Moving between online tiers is immediate.
2. **Copy Blob** to a new blob in the target tier (`az storage blob copy start --tier`). Microsoft recommends it for **rehydrating from archive** and for moving **cool or cold to hot**, because the source keeps its tier and you avoid the early deletion charge on it.
3. A **lifecycle management policy** (next lesson), which can move blobs cooler and delete them but **can't rehydrate** from archive.

You can also upload straight into a tier (`az storage blob upload --tier Cool`, `azcopy copy --block-blob-tier Archive`).

**Portal:** container > select blobs > **Change tier** > choose the tier (and rehydrate priority for archive).

```bash
az storage blob set-tier --account-name stcontosodata01 --container-name reports \
  --name 2025/annual.pdf --tier Cold --auth-mode login
```

```powershell
$ctx  = New-AzStorageContext -StorageAccountName stcontosodata01 -UseConnectedAccount
$blob = Get-AzStorageBlob -Container reports -Blob 2025/annual.pdf -Context $ctx
$blob.BlobClient.SetAccessTier("Cold", $null)
```

## The archive tier in detail

A blob in archive **can't be read or modified**; only its metadata and properties are available. Restrictions:

- Archive is supported only in accounts with **LRS, GRS or RA-GRS**. **ZRS, GZRS and RA-GZRS accounts can't archive.**
- Blobs that use an **encryption scope** can't be archived.
- An account holding archived blobs must rehydrate (or move) them before converting to a zone-redundant option.

### Rehydration

To read an archived blob, bring it back to an online tier (hot, cool or cold):

| Method | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Set Blob Tier** on the archived blob | The same blob moves to the online tier. Early deletion applies if it was in archive less than 180 days |
| **Copy Blob** to a new name or container in an online tier | The archived source stays in archive; the copy becomes readable. Avoids the early deletion charge. Destination must be in the same account or another account in the same region |

Two **rehydration priorities** (header `x-ms-rehydrate-priority`):

| Priority | Microsoft Learn guidance |
|---|---|
| **Standard** (default) | May take **up to 15 hours** for objects under 10 GB |
| **High** | May complete in **under one hour** for objects under 10 GB; costs more |

You can raise a pending standard request to high, but not lower it. While rehydrating, the blob's **archive status** shows `rehydrate-pending-to-hot` (or cool, cold) and it is billed as archive until the tier changes. Subscribe to the **BlobTierChanged** Event Grid event to know when it is done. Microsoft Learn also notes an account-level rehydration throughput limit, so bulk rehydration of large volumes takes longer than the per-blob guidance suggests.

```bash
# Rehydrate in place, urgently
az storage blob set-tier --account-name stcontosodata01 --container-name archive \
  --name 2019/ledger.zip --tier Hot --rehydrate-priority High --auth-mode login

# Or copy out of archive into a new online blob
az storage blob copy start --account-name stcontosodata01 --auth-mode login \
  --source-container archive --source-blob 2019/ledger.zip \
  --destination-container restore --destination-blob 2019/ledger.zip \
  --tier Hot --rehydrate-priority Standard
```

```powershell
Start-AzStorageBlobCopy -SrcContainer archive -SrcBlob 2019/ledger.zip -Context $ctx `
  -DestContainer restore -DestBlob 2019/ledger.zip -StandardBlobTier Hot -RehydratePriority Standard
```

> **Watch out:** A lifecycle rule such as "tierToArchive after 90 days since modification" will push a blob you just rehydrated straight back to archive, because rehydration doesn't change the last-modified time. Either copy the blob out instead of rehydrating in place, or add `daysAfterLastTierChangeGreaterThan` to the rule.

## Choosing a tier

| Requirement | Tier |
|---|---|
| Data read many times a day | Hot |
| Kept at least a month, read a few times | Cool |
| Kept at least three months, rarely read, must open instantly | Cold |
| Kept six months or more, can wait hours to read | Archive |
| Must be readable within minutes after years of storage | Cold (not archive) |
| Account is ZRS or GZRS and data must be cheapest possible | Cold (archive unavailable) |

## Try it yourself

Azure Cloud Shell. Cost: a few cents. Archive early-deletion charges on a few bytes are negligible.

```bash
rg=rg-lab-d2tier; sa=stlabtier$RANDOM
az group create -n $rg -l eastus
az storage account create -n $sa -g $rg --sku Standard_LRS --kind StorageV2 --access-tier Hot
key=$(az storage account keys list -g $rg -n $sa --query [0].value -o tsv)
az storage container create --account-name $sa --account-key $key -n data
for t in Hot Cool Cold Archive; do echo $t > $t.txt; az storage blob upload --account-name $sa --account-key $key -c data -f $t.txt -n $t.txt --tier $t -o none; done
az storage blob list --account-name $sa --account-key $key -c data --query "[].{name:name, tier:properties.blobTier, inferred:properties.blobTierInferred}" -o table
az storage blob download --account-name $sa --account-key $key -c data -n Archive.txt -f out.txt   # fails: archived
az storage blob set-tier --account-name $sa --account-key $key -c data -n Archive.txt --tier Hot --rehydrate-priority High
az storage blob show --account-name $sa --account-key $key -c data -n Archive.txt --query properties.rehydrationStatus
```

In the portal, open the blob and look at **Access tier** and **Archive status**. Try creating a **ZRS** account and setting a blob to Archive to see the refusal. Clean up: `az group delete -n $rg --yes --no-wait`.

## Key takeaways

- Hot (no minimum), cool (30 days), cold (90 days), archive (180 days, offline); early deletion is prorated.
- Tiers apply to block blobs in GPv2 (and legacy Blob Storage); not to append, page or premium block blobs.
- Account default tier: hot, cool or cold, never archive.
- Archive only with LRS, GRS, RA-GRS; not with encryption scopes.
- Rehydrate with Set Blob Tier or (preferably) Copy Blob; Standard up to 15 hours, High under one hour for blobs under 10 GB.
- Lifecycle policies can archive but never rehydrate.
