## Awareness as a control

People are targeted because they can open doors that technology keeps shut: approving a fraudulent payment, reading out an MFA code, plugging in a found USB device. Security awareness is a managerial and operational control whose goal is **changed behavior**, not completed courses. NIST SP 800-50 Rev. 1 (September 2024) frames it as a Cybersecurity and Privacy Learning Program (CPLP) managed through a life cycle of four phases: **plan and strategy, analysis and design, development and implementation, assessment and improvement**. SP 800-53 Rev. 5 supplies the controls: AT-2 (literacy training and awareness for all users), AT-3 (role-based training) and PL-4 (rules of behavior with acknowledgment).

## Types of training

| Type | When | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Initial/onboarding** | Before or when access is granted | New-hire module on the AUP, reporting incidents, password manager, phishing basics; AT-2 requires training "as part of initial training for new users" |
| **Ongoing** | At a set frequency and when systems or threats change | Annual refresher plus short monthly micro-lessons; AT-2 says later training "may be satisfied by one or more short ad hoc sessions" on recent attack schemes and policy changes |
| **Targeted** | For specific roles or high-risk groups | Payment-fraud scenarios for accounts payable; deepfake call-back drills for executive assistants; secure coding for developers (role-based training, AT-3) |
| **Corrective** | After a specific failure | A short lesson delivered to a user who submitted credentials in a phishing simulation; a refresher for a team after a policy violation |

AT-2 also requires that training content be updated after events and that lessons learned "from internal or external security incidents or breaches" be incorporated. SP 800-50 Rev. 1 is explicit that phishing exercises "should not be punitive, nor should any employee be called out for their response"; corrective training is a learning step, not a punishment. Disciplinary action, where policy provides for it, is a separate HR matter for repeated or deliberate violations.

> **Exam tip:** First day = **onboarding**. Every year = **ongoing**. Only the finance team, because they handle payments = **targeted**. Because this person just clicked = **corrective**.

## Delivery mechanisms

- **Learning management system (LMS):** hosts courses, assigns them by role, tracks completion and quiz scores, and produces the records auditors ask for.
- **Self-service portals:** on-demand resources people use when they need them: a security intranet page, how-to guides, a "report a phish" button, short videos, policy library, an internal chatbot.
- **One-to-one:** individual coaching: a security champion walking a new administrator through privileged-access rules, or a conversation after a repeated simulation failure. High impact, low scale.
- **One-to-many:** a presenter to a group: town halls, lunch-and-learns, webinars, tabletop exercises, awareness posters and login banners (AT-2's awareness techniques include "displaying posters", "displaying logon screen messages" and "generating email advisories").

## Reporting and monitoring effectiveness

### Metrics

SP 800-50 Rev. 1 lists quantitative program data such as attendance and completion rates, "phishing or other simulated attack response tracking", "metrics on incident reporting that demonstrate employee ability to recognize and report potential cybersecurity events", and "employee testing data before the learning program, immediately after the learning program, and three months after" to check retention. Its example metric for behavior change is the percentage of participants who received a phishing test email "and reported it appropriately".

For phishing simulations:

| Metric | Formula | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| **Click rate** (failure rate) | Users who clicked ÷ emails delivered | Lower is better |
| **Credential-submission rate** | Users who entered credentials ÷ emails delivered | Lower is better; the most serious failure |
| **Report rate** | Users who reported the email ÷ emails delivered | Higher is better |
| **Time to first report** | Time from delivery to the first report | Shorter is better |

The **report rate** matters most because it measures the behavior that protects everyone: a real phishing campaign usually reaches many people, and one early report lets the SOC pull the message from every mailbox and block the sender before others click. A low click rate can also come from people simply ignoring email; only reporting shows active recognition. Compare rates across campaigns of similar difficulty (the NIST Phish Scale helps rate difficulty), not across an easy and a hard lure.

### Worked example

A campaign reaches 400 users. 48 click, 12 of those submit credentials, 92 report it.

- Click rate = 48 ÷ 400 = **12%**
- Credential-submission rate = 12 ÷ 400 = **3%**
- Report rate = 92 ÷ 400 = **23%**

The 12 credential submitters get corrective training; if finance is overrepresented among them, finance gets targeted training next quarter.

### Managerial reports and risk scoring

- **Managerial reports:** summaries for managers and executives: completion by department, overdue training, simulation trends, incidents attributable to human error, reporting volumes. They make managers accountable for their teams and feed the risk register.
- **Personnel behavior risk scoring:** the objective does not define this term. The most defensible reading, matching how "human risk management" products describe it, is a score per person or group built from observed behavior (simulation results, training completion, policy violations, risky actions flagged by DLP or the secure email gateway, privileged access held) so that the people with the highest risk get targeted training and extra controls first. Scoring individuals raises privacy and employee-relations questions, so involve HR, legal and privacy, and be transparent about what is measured. No question here depends on a particular scoring method.

## Common training topics

| Topic | What people need to do |
|---|---|
| **Social engineering** | Recognize phishing, smishing, vishing, quishing, pretexting and impersonation (Domain 2.5); verify through a known channel; report |
| **Emerging security topics** | Deepfake audio and video, AI-generated lures, MFA fatigue prompts, safe use of AI tools with company data (2.6) |
| **Password and credential management** | Use a password manager, unique long passphrases, MFA and passkeys; never share codes; report suspected exposure |
| **Remote work / teleworking / hybrid** | Secure home Wi-Fi, VPN or ZTNA use, screen privacy in public places, physical security of devices, family members and work devices |
| **BYOD** | Enroll devices as the BYOD policy requires, keep work data in the managed container, update the OS, report loss at once |
| **Business email compromise (BEC)** | Verify any change of bank details or urgent payment request by calling a known number; watch for look-alike domains and reply-to tricks |
| **Removable media and cables** | Do not plug in found USB drives; use only issued media; be aware that malicious USB **cables** and chargers can contain hidden implants |
| **Situational awareness** | Notice tailgaters, unfamiliar people in secure areas, shoulder surfers, unusual requests; challenge or report |
| **Operational security (OPSEC)** | Do not reveal internal details (system names, travel plans of executives, project names, badge photos) on social media or in public; AT-2 lists operations security as required content |

> **Real world:** Measure the cost of each topic in terms of real incidents: if BEC losses are the largest line in the risk register (5.2), BEC verification belongs in onboarding, in finance's targeted training, and in a quarterly simulation, and its success metric is "zero unverified bank-detail changes".

## Try it yourself

Paper exercise. Design a 12-month awareness plan for a 300-person Contoso office. For each quarter, list one campaign (topic and delivery mechanism), the training type (onboarding, ongoing, targeted, corrective), and the metric you will report to management with a target value. Then compute: a simulation sent to 300 people gets 27 clicks and 84 reports. What are the click and report rates? (Answers: 9% and 28%.)

## Key takeaways

- Training types: initial/onboarding, ongoing (periodic and event-driven), targeted (role-based or high-risk groups), corrective (after a failure, non-punitive).
- Delivery: LMS (assign and track), self-service portals (on demand), one-to-one (coaching), one-to-many (sessions, campaigns).
- Effectiveness: click rate down, report rate and speed up, completion and retention tracked; managerial reports create accountability; behavior risk scoring prioritizes who needs more help.
- Report rate is the key phishing metric: early reports let responders contain a campaign for everyone.
- Core topics: social engineering, emerging threats, credentials, remote/hybrid work, BYOD, BEC, removable media and cables, situational awareness and OPSEC.
