## Audit, assessment, examination, attestation

These words overlap in everyday use, so anchor them to their sources:

| Term | Meaning | Typical output |
|---|---|---|
| **Audit** | "Systematic, independent and documented process for obtaining audit evidence and evaluating it objectively to determine the extent to which the audit criteria are fulfilled" (NIST glossary, from ISO 30401). Its note: an audit can be **internal** (first party, "conducted by the organization itself, or by an external party on its behalf") or **external** (second or third party) | Findings against stated criteria; often a formal opinion |
| **Assessment** | Determining how effectively the thing assessed "meets specific security objectives" (SP 800-115), using testing, examination and interviewing | Findings, risk ratings and recommendations; no formal opinion required |
| **Examination** | In SP 800-115, checking, inspecting, reviewing or analyzing objects (documents, configurations, logs). In accounting, an **examination** is the attestation engagement in which a CPA gives an opinion, as in a SOC 2 examination | Evidence, or an independent opinion |
| **Attestation** | A statement that specified requirements have been fulfilled (ISO/IEC 17000 via NIST): by an independent party (SOC 2 report) or by management itself (self-attestation) | A signed statement or report |

> **Exam tip:** Independent, against defined criteria, producing an opinion: **audit**. Evaluating how well controls reduce risk and recommending improvements: **assessment**. A signed statement that requirements are met: **attestation**.

## Data gathering

SP 800-115 names three assessment methods: **testing** ("exercising one or more assessment objects under specified conditions to compare actual and expected behaviors"), **examination**, and **interviewing**. The SY0-801 list adds the ways auditors collect evidence:

- **Sampling:** testing a subset of a population (20 of 1,400 user accounts, 25 of 300 change tickets) and drawing a conclusion about the whole. Random or statistical sampling supports a conclusion about the population; judgmental sampling targets high-risk items. A sample that is too small or not random cannot support "all accounts are reviewed".
- **Questionnaires/surveys:** structured self-reported answers, efficient at scale (vendor questionnaires, control self-assessment surveys) but weak evidence until verified.
- **Interviews:** discussions with control owners and staff to understand how a process really works and where evidence lives (SP 800-115's "interviewing").
- **Assertion:** a claim by the party being audited that a control exists or a requirement is met (management's assertion in a SOC 2 report is the formal example). The auditor's job is to gather evidence that supports or refutes each assertion; an assertion alone is not evidence.

### Reference sources

Auditors and assessors use threat models as reference sources: to check that controls address how attacks actually happen, and to describe findings in common terms.

**MITRE ATT&CK** is a knowledge base of adversary behavior observed in real intrusions, in Enterprise, Mobile and ICS matrices. **Tactics** are "the 'why'", "the adversary's tactical goal"; **techniques** are how a goal is achieved; sub-techniques are more specific; entries also list procedure examples, groups, software, mitigations and detections. As of ATT&CK v20 (October 2026) Enterprise has **15 tactics**: Reconnaissance, Resource Development, Initial Access, Execution, Persistence, Privilege Escalation, **Stealth**, **Defense Impairment**, Credential Access, Discovery, Lateral Movement, Collection, Command and Control, Exfiltration and Impact. ATT&CK v19 (April 2026) split the former Defense Evasion tactic into Stealth and Defense Impairment, so older material listing 14 tactics with Defense Evasion is out of date. Tactics are not a strict sequence. Use: map detections and controls to techniques to find coverage gaps; describe a red-team finding as "T1566 Phishing".

**The Cyber Kill Chain** (from Lockheed Martin's paper "Intelligence-Driven Computer Network Defense Informed by Analysis of Adversary Campaigns and Intrusion Kill Chains") models an intrusion as seven phases an attacker must complete: **reconnaissance, weaponization, delivery, exploitation, installation, command and control (C2), actions on objectives**. Breaking any link stops the intrusion. The paper's course-of-action matrix maps defenses per phase to six actions taken from information-operations doctrine: **detect, deny, disrupt, degrade, deceive, destroy** (for example, at exploitation: HIDS detects, patching denies, DEP disrupts). It is linear and perimeter-focused, which is why ATT&CK is often used alongside it.

**The Diamond Model of Intrusion Analysis** (a 2013 paper released for public use through the U.S. Defense Technical Information Center) describes every intrusion event by four core features: **adversary, capability, infrastructure, victim**. Axiom 1: "for every intrusion event there exists an adversary taking a step towards an intended goal by using a capability over infrastructure against a victim to produce a result." Meta-features add timestamp, phase, result, direction, methodology and resources. Its strength is **pivoting**: from a known piece of infrastructure (a C2 domain) to the capability seen using it, to other victims, to the adversary.

| Model | Unit of analysis | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| ATT&CK | Tactics and techniques (behaviors) | Detection coverage, control mapping, red-team planning |
| Cyber Kill Chain | Seven sequential phases | Where to break an attack; defense-in-depth per phase |
| Diamond Model | One event's four features and their relationships | Analysis and pivoting between related events and attribution |

## Scoping

- **Audit charter:** the formal document that establishes an (internal) audit function's purpose, authority and responsibilities: its right to access records, people and systems, its reporting line (usually to the board's audit committee, for independence), and its scope. Individual engagements then get an engagement letter or plan stating objectives, scope, criteria and timing.
- **Frequency:** how often each area is audited, set by risk, regulation and contracts: annually for PCI DSS assessments and SOC 2 reports, more often for high-risk areas, and after major changes. Continuous control monitoring can supplement but not replace periodic independent audits.

## Engagement types

| Type | Who performs it | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Gap analysis** | Internal team or consultant | Compare the current state with a target standard or framework and list what is missing; NIST SP 800-50 Rev. 1's glossary entry describes it as comparing current performance "with the desired, expected performance". Usually the first step before seeking certification |
| **Internal: compliance** | Internal compliance function | Check that the organization follows its policies and obligations |
| **Internal: audit committee** | Internal audit, reporting to the board's audit committee | Independent assurance to the board on controls and risk; the committee oversees audit and approves its plan |
| **Internal: self-assessments** | Control owners themselves | Owners rate their own controls (control self-assessment); cheap and frequent, but not independent |
| **External: examinations** | Independent CPA firm | Attestation engagements producing an opinion (SOC 1, SOC 2 examinations) |
| **External: assessments** | Qualified assessors or consultants | Evaluation against a standard, such as a PCI DSS assessment by a Qualified Security Assessor or an external penetration test |
| **External: regulatory** | A regulator or its appointed examiners | Check compliance with laws and regulations; findings can lead to sanctions |
| **External: independent third-party audits** | An accredited, independent audit firm | Certification audits (ISO/IEC 27001 certification by an accredited certification body), audits required by contract |
| **Benchmarking** | Internal or external | Compare your practices or metrics with peers, industry data or a maturity model |

## Frameworks and standards

| Kind | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Industry-based** | PCI DSS (anyone that stores, processes or transmits cardholder data, per PCI SSC), NERC CIP (North American bulk electric system), HITRUST CSF (healthcare focus) | Set by an industry body or sector regulator for one sector |
| **International** | ISO/IEC 27001 (requirements for an information security management system; organizations can be certified against it), ISO/IEC 27002 (guidance on the controls), ISO/IEC 27701 (privacy), ISO 31000 (risk management) | Usable in any country; certification by accredited bodies |
| **Region-specific** | GDPR (EU/EEA), CCPA/CPRA (California), state breach-notification laws | Apply because of where people or data are |

NIST frameworks (CSF 2.0, SP 800-53) are U.S. government publications that are voluntary for most private organizations and widely used internationally; questions should give you the deciding detail if one is meant.

## Functional and behavioral testing

The objectives list these without defining them, and no primary source defines them for audits. The most defensible reading:

- **Functional testing** checks that a control **does what it is designed to do**: the badge system really denies a revoked badge; a restore from backup produces usable data; the firewall drops the traffic the rule set says it drops. It compares actual with expected behavior, which is SP 800-115's definition of testing.
- **Behavioral testing** checks how **people (or systems) actually behave** under realistic conditions: whether staff challenge a tailgater, report a phishing simulation, follow the change procedure when no one is watching, or whether a system behaves securely under unusual inputs and load.

No question in this course depends on the exact boundary between the two.

## Try it yourself

Paper exercise. Take one control you know well (for example, "terminated users are disabled within 24 hours"). Write: the assertion management would make; a sampling plan (population, sample size, how you pick); two interview questions; one functional test; one behavioral test. Then map one attack that would exploit a failure of the control to an ATT&CK tactic and a Kill Chain phase.

## Key takeaways

- Audit: independent, criteria-based, often an opinion. Assessment: evaluates effectiveness and recommends. Attestation: a statement that requirements are met. Examination: an attestation engagement (SOC 2) or SP 800-115's review technique.
- Data gathering: sampling, questionnaires/surveys, interviews, and verifying assertions with evidence.
- ATT&CK: tactics (why) and techniques (how); 15 Enterprise tactics in v20, with Stealth and Defense Impairment replacing Defense Evasion. Kill Chain: seven phases. Diamond: adversary, capability, infrastructure, victim.
- Scoping: the audit charter gives authority and independence; frequency follows risk and regulation.
- Engagements: gap analysis; internal (compliance, audit committee, self-assessment); external (examinations, assessments, regulatory, independent third-party audits); benchmarking.
- Frameworks: industry-based (PCI DSS, NERC CIP), international (ISO/IEC 27001/27002), region-specific (GDPR, CCPA).
