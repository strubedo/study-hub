## Why asset management is a security control

You cannot protect, patch, monitor or wipe what you do not know you have. Unknown servers miss patches, unknown laptops miss EDR, unknown SaaS subscriptions hold company data nobody reviews, and unknown drives leave the building with data on them. NIST SP 800-40 Rev. 4 puts it at the start of patch management: you need to know what assets you use and what software and versions they run, "down to the level of packages and libraries", before you can know which new vulnerabilities affect you. Objective 4.2 covers **hardware, software and data** assets through their whole life cycle.

## The asset management life cycle

The objectives list the stages in order:

1. **Planning/scoping**
2. **Acquisition/procurement process**
3. **Assignment/accounting**
4. **Monitoring/asset tracking**
5. **Disposal/decommissioning**

Each stage has its own security implications.

### 1. Planning and scoping

Decide what is needed, why, and what the security requirements are before anything is bought or built:

- What data will the asset hold, and at what classification (Domain 3, 3.3)? That decides encryption, location and disposal requirements later.
- What is the scope of the asset program: on-premises hardware, cloud resources, SaaS, mobile devices, IoT/OT, software licenses, data sets?
- What standards apply: hardening baselines, approved models and operating systems, support life cycle (do not plan to buy hardware or software that reaches end of support during its intended life).
- Capacity and budget, including the cost of securing and eventually disposing of the asset.

### 2. Acquisition and procurement

Security requirements belong in the purchasing process, not after delivery:

- **Approved vendors and products**: buy from vetted suppliers and authorized resellers to reduce counterfeit and tampered hardware (Domain 2 supply chain vectors). Third-party risk assessment is Domain 5 (5.3).
- **Security requirements in contracts**: patch and support commitments, vulnerability disclosure, data handling, right to audit, end-of-life dates. SP 800-40 Rev. 4 has a section on considering software maintenance in procurement.
- **Software bill of materials (SBOM)** where available, so you know the components you are importing.
- **Licensing**: legitimate licenses (pirated software is a malware vector and a legal risk); track license counts.
- **Shadow IT prevention**: route all purchases, including SaaS sign-ups on a credit card, through a process that registers the asset.

### 3. Assignment and accounting

Once the asset arrives, it is recorded and given to someone:

- **Inventory record** in a configuration management database (CMDB) or asset register: asset tag, serial number, model, MAC addresses, OS and version, location, purchase date, warranty and support end date, classification of data it holds.
- **Ownership**: an accountable **owner** (a business role that decides how the asset is used and protected) and often a separate **custodian** or operator who runs it day to day. Data roles (owner, custodian, steward, controller) are covered in Domain 3 (3.3).
- **Assignment to users**: who has the laptop, phone or token, with a signed acknowledgment of the acceptable use policy. This is how a lost device is traced, how assets are recovered at offboarding, and how responsibility is established.
- **Classification**: label the asset with the highest classification of data it stores or processes.
- **Accounting**: financial tracking (cost, depreciation) and license reconciliation; an asset in the books but missing from the floor is a finding.

### 4. Monitoring and asset tracking

The inventory is only useful if it stays accurate:

- **Discovery** compares what is actually on the network (network scans, DHCP and ARP tables, switch CAM tables, NAC logs, EDR consoles, cloud APIs) with what the inventory says. Anything unknown is investigated: it may be shadow IT or a rogue device.
- **Agents and MDM** report hardware and software inventory from endpoints, including installed applications and versions.
- **Tags**: barcodes, QR codes and RFID tags for physical tracking; GPS or MDM location for mobile devices.
- **Software inventory and license monitoring**: unapproved software, expired licenses, end-of-support versions.
- **Change tracking**: moves, reassignment, configuration changes (Domain 1, 1.2), and periodic audits or reconciliations.
- **Cloud assets** are created and destroyed by API calls in minutes, so cloud inventory has to be automated (tags enforced by policy, CSPM tools, lesson 4.3).

> **Exam tip:** A vulnerability scan or NAC report shows a device that is not in the CMDB. The asset management failure is in **monitoring/asset tracking** (and possibly procurement, if someone bought it outside the process). The first step is to identify and locate it, then either register and secure it or remove it.

### 5. Disposal and decommissioning

Retiring an asset is where data most often leaks:

- **Decommissioning**: remove the system from service in a controlled way: migrate or archive data according to the retention policy (some data must be kept, and data under legal hold must not be destroyed), revoke its certificates, accounts and API keys, remove DNS records and firewall rules that reference it, update the CMDB and monitoring.
- **Sanitization**: NIST SP 800-88 (Rev. 2, September 2025, "Guidelines for Media Sanitization") defines three methods: **clear** (logical techniques such as overwriting, protecting against simple non-invasive recovery), **purge** (techniques that make recovery infeasible even with laboratory methods, such as cryptographic erase or a device's built-in sanitize command) and **destroy** (shredding, disintegration, incineration, making the media unusable). The choice depends on the data's sensitivity and whether the media will be reused or leave the organization.
- **Documentation**: SP 800-88 includes a sample **certificate of sanitization**; third-party disposal vendors provide a **certificate of destruction**. Keep these as evidence, along with the serial numbers of the media.
- **Environmental and legal requirements**: e-waste regulations, data retention laws, contract terms for leased equipment (leased devices go back to the lessor, so sanitize before returning them).
- **Software and data**: uninstall and reclaim licenses; delete data from every copy, including backups and cloud replicas, according to the retention schedule.

> **Watch out:** "Formatting" a drive or deleting files is not sanitization; the data remains recoverable. Also, overwriting is not reliable on SSDs because of wear leveling and spare blocks; use purge methods (the drive's sanitize or cryptographic erase command) or destruction.

## Hardware, software and data: what to track

| Asset type | What to record | Security implication if missing |
|---|---|---|
| Hardware | Tag, serial, model, location, owner, firmware, support end date | Unpatched or unmonitored devices; lost devices not wiped |
| Software | Product, version, license, install base, support end date, SBOM | Unknown vulnerable versions; license violations; end-of-life software |
| Data | Location, classification, owner, retention period | Data left unprotected, retained too long, or not found when a breach or legal request arrives |
| Cloud/SaaS | Account, subscription, owner, region, tags | Shadow IT; data in unapproved locations; orphaned resources |

## Try it yourself

On any computer, list the assets you personally own as if you were their asset manager: each device (model, serial number from the device's settings or label, OS version, where its data is backed up), the software subscriptions you pay for, and where your important data lives. For each, write what you would do at disposal. On Windows, `msinfo32` and Settings > System > About show model and serial details; on macOS, Apple menu > About This Mac; on Linux, `hostnamectl` and the `dmidecode` man page (reading the man page is enough).

## Key takeaways

- Life cycle stages in order: planning/scoping, acquisition/procurement, assignment/accounting, monitoring/asset tracking, disposal/decommissioning.
- Put security requirements (support life, patching, SBOM, approved vendors) into procurement, not after delivery.
- Every asset needs an inventory record, an accountable owner and, for user devices, an assigned user.
- Continuous discovery reconciles reality with the inventory; unknown devices point to shadow IT or rogue hardware.
- Decommission deliberately: retain or archive data per policy and legal hold, revoke credentials, remove rules, update the CMDB.
- SP 800-88 Rev. 2: clear, purge, destroy; document with a certificate of sanitization or destruction. Formatting is not sanitization.
