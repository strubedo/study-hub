## Why RF basics are on a FortiAP exam

Every FortiAP profile setting you will touch later — `band`, `channel`, `channel-bonding`, `power-mode`, `zero-wait-dfs`, the country code — is a knob on the physics covered here. Fortinet's exam expects you to read a radio configuration and know whether it is legal, sensible and compatible with the clients. This lesson covers the 802.11 standards, the three bands, channel planning, DFS and the regulatory domain.

## 802.11 amendments and Wi-Fi generations

The IEEE writes the 802.11 amendments; the Wi-Fi Alliance certifies products and, since 2018, brands them with generation numbers. The generation names were applied to 802.11n and later only.

| Amendment | Wi-Fi Alliance name | Bands | Key additions |
|-----------|--------------------|-------|---------------|
| 802.11b (1999) | none | 2.4 GHz | DSSS/CCK, 22 MHz channels, max 11 Mbps |
| 802.11a (1999) | none | 5 GHz | OFDM, 20 MHz channels, max 54 Mbps |
| 802.11g (2003) | none | 2.4 GHz | OFDM in 2.4 GHz, max 54 Mbps, backward compatible with 802.11b |
| 802.11n (2009) | Wi-Fi 4 | 2.4 and 5 GHz | MIMO (up to 4 spatial streams), 40 MHz channels, frame aggregation; max 600 Mbps |
| 802.11ac (2013) | Wi-Fi 5 | 5 GHz only | 80 and 160 MHz channels, 256-QAM, downlink MU-MIMO |
| 802.11ax (2021) | Wi-Fi 6 | 2.4 and 5 GHz | OFDMA, 1024-QAM, uplink and downlink MU-MIMO, BSS coloring, target wake time |
| 802.11ax in 6 GHz | Wi-Fi 6E | 6 GHz | Same PHY as Wi-Fi 6, new spectrum; WPA3 or OWE mandatory |
| 802.11be (2024) | Wi-Fi 7 | 2.4, 5 and 6 GHz | 320 MHz channels (6 GHz only), 4096-QAM, multi-link operation (MLO) |

Data rates quoted above are each standard's theoretical maximum PHY rate; real throughput is roughly half or less. Don't memorise rates beyond b/a/g/n — the exam cares far more about bands and features.

> **Exam tip:** 802.11ac is **5 GHz only** in the IEEE standard. FortiOS still offers a `802.11ac-2G` band value on some radios (a vendor extension using 802.11ac-style rates in 2.4 GHz), so don't be surprised to see it in a `band` list.

> **Watch out:** "Wi-Fi 6E" is not a new IEEE amendment. It is 802.11ax operating in 6 GHz. In FortiOS that is `set band 802.11ax-6G`.

### How FortiOS names bands

A FortiAP radio's `band` value pairs the amendment with the band. The FortiOS 7.6 CLI Reference lists: `802.11a`, `802.11b`, `802.11g`, `802.11n-2G`, `802.11n-5G`, `802.11ac-2G`, `802.11ac-5G`, `802.11ax-2G`, `802.11ax-5G`, `802.11ax-6G`, `802.11be-2G`, `802.11be-5G` and `802.11be-6G`. The radio hardware limits which ones you can pick — a Wi-Fi 6 FortiAP has no `be` options, and only a 6 GHz-capable radio shows the `-6G` values.

## The three bands

### 2.4 GHz

* Channels are numbered 1–14 at 5 MHz spacing, but each transmission is about 20 MHz (OFDM) or 22 MHz (DSSS) wide, so neighbours overlap.
* Allowed channels depend on the country: 1–11 in the United States, 1–13 in most of Europe; channel 14 is a Japan-only 802.11b relic.
* Only **1, 6 and 11** are non-overlapping at 20 MHz in a 1–11 domain. That is FortiOS's **Three Channels** plan for a 2.4 GHz radio. FortiOS also offers **Four Channels** (1, 4, 8, 11, whose neighbours overlap slightly) and **Custom**.
* 40 MHz in 2.4 GHz consumes most of the band and leaves no clean three-channel reuse. Keep 2.4 GHz radios at 20 MHz (FortiOS `channel-bonding` default is `20MHz`).
* Lots of non-Wi-Fi interference: microwave ovens, Bluetooth, cordless phones.

### 5 GHz

| Sub-band | Channels (20 MHz) | Notes |
|----------|-------------------|-------|
| UNII-1 | 36, 40, 44, 48 | No radar detection required |
| UNII-2A | 52, 56, 60, 64 | **DFS** required |
| UNII-2C (extended) | 100–144 | **DFS** required; 120–128 overlap weather radar (extra restrictions in many countries) |
| UNII-3 | 149, 153, 157, 161, 165 | No DFS in the US |
| UNII-4 | 169, 173, 177 | Newer allocation; not allowed in every country |

More spectrum means more non-overlapping 20/40/80 MHz channels, which is why 5 GHz carries most enterprise clients. Common practice is 20 or 40 MHz in dense deployments and 80 MHz where AP density is low.

**UNII-4 on FortiAP:** FortiAP G-series in **Single 5G** platform mode can use 169, 173 and 177; they are not available in Dual 5G mode. With dedicated scan enabled they are available by default; with dedicated scan disabled you enable them with `set unii-4-5ghz-band enable` (default disable) in the FortiAP profile, which removes the UNII-5/UNII-6 channels from radio 3.

### 6 GHz

* In the US the band runs 5.925–7.125 GHz: 1200 MHz, 59 channels of 20 MHz, room for seven 160 MHz channels or three non-overlapping 320 MHz channels. Other regions open less of it — always check the country.
* Only Wi-Fi 6E and Wi-Fi 7 devices use it, so there are no legacy clients slowing the air down.
* Security rule (Wi-Fi Alliance): **WPA3 or OWE only, with PMF required**. No open and no WPA2 SSIDs. WPA3-SAE in 6 GHz must use **H2E** (hash-to-element). This is why a FortiOS mesh backhaul over 6 GHz must be WPA3-SAE with `sae-h2e-only enable`.
* 320 MHz channels are a Wi-Fi 7 feature and exist only in 6 GHz; FortiOS uses `channel-bonding 320MHz` plus `channel-bonding-ext 320MHz-1|320MHz-2` to pick one of the two overlapping 320 MHz channel sets.

## Channel width

Doubling the width roughly doubles the peak rate but halves the number of non-overlapping channels and raises the noise floor (wider receivers hear more noise). In FortiOS, `channel-bonding` on each radio accepts `20MHz`, `40MHz`, `80MHz`, `160MHz`, `240MHz` and `320MHz` (default `20MHz`); the radio and band limit the real choices.

> **Real world:** In an office with many APs, 5 GHz at 40 MHz and 2.4 GHz at 20 MHz is a safe start. Turn on wide channels only after a survey shows clean spectrum.

## DFS — dynamic frequency selection

UNII-2A and UNII-2C share spectrum with radar. The regulators (FCC, ETSI and others) require an AP that wants a DFS channel to:

1. **Listen first** — a Channel Availability Check (CAC), normally 60 seconds (ETSI requires 10 minutes on the weather-radar channels 120–128).
2. **Keep listening** while in service.
3. **Leave quickly** when radar is detected (the channel move must finish within 10 seconds) and avoid that channel for at least 30 minutes (non-occupancy period).

Clients on a channel that hits radar are pushed to a new channel, which they feel as a short outage. That is why some designs exclude DFS channels near airports or ports, and why DARRP has `include-dfs-channel` (default enable) in the ARRP profile.

FortiOS has a per-radio `zero-wait-dfs` setting (default **enable**) intended to cut the waiting time when a radio moves onto a DFS channel; the CLI Reference describes it only as "zero wait DFS", so learn the keyword and its default rather than implementation details.

## Regulatory domain and the country code

Which channels and how much power are legal depends on the country. The FortiGate controller defaults to the **United States**. Set the country before you build profiles:

```text
FGT-HQ # config wireless-controller setting
FGT-HQ (setting) # set country FR
FGT-HQ (setting) # end
```

* Fortinet's guide says to remove all FortiAP profiles before changing the country.
* A FortiAP profile has its own `ap-country`; its default `NA` means "use the country configured for the VDOM".
* On a FortiAP, `cw_diag -c all-countries` lists the country codes, region codes and regulatory domains.
* The profile's indoor/outdoor setting (`indoor-outdoor-deployment`, default `platform-determined`) also changes the permitted channel list, because some channels are indoor-only.

## Power: dBm, EIRP and signal metrics

* **dBm** is power relative to 1 mW: 0 dBm = 1 mW, 10 dBm = 10 mW, 20 dBm = 100 mW. +3 dB doubles power, +10 dB multiplies it by ten.
* **EIRP** (effective isotropic radiated power) = transmitter power + antenna gain − cable loss. Regulators limit EIRP, not just radio output. FortiOS radio power is expressed as EIRP: `power-mode` is `percentage` (default, `power-level` 0–100, default 100) or `dBm` (`power-value` 1–33, default 27). 100% means the maximum the region and AP allow.
* **RSSI** — received signal strength, a negative dBm value at the receiver (−55 dBm is strong, −80 dBm is weak).
* **Noise floor** — background RF energy, typically around −90 to −95 dBm on a clean channel.
* **SNR** = signal − noise, in dB. A client at −65 dBm on a −95 dBm noise floor has 30 dB SNR. Higher SNR allows higher modulation (MCS) and data rates.

> **Exam tip:** Higher AP power is not always better. Clients transmit at lower power than APs; an AP that shouts too loudly creates cells where the client hears the AP but the AP cannot hear the client, and it raises co-channel interference with neighbouring APs.

## Other radio features you will see in FortiAP profiles

* **BSS coloring** (802.11ax): a 6-bit colour (`bss-color` 1–63, 0 = disabled; `bss-color-mode auto` by default) lets radios ignore overlapping transmissions from other BSSs on the same channel.
* **Short guard interval** (`short-guard-interval`): 400 ns instead of 800 ns for 802.11n/ac, a small rate gain.
* **Beacon interval** (`beacon-interval`, default 100): the standard measures it in time units (1 TU = 1.024 ms), so 100 is about 102 ms.

## Lab it yourself

You can do the controller-side part on the FortiGate-VM trial; radio behaviour needs a FortiAP or FortiWiFi.

1. On the VM, run `config wireless-controller setting`, then `set country ?` to see the code list, and `get` to see the current country.
2. Create a FortiAP profile for a model you know (`config wireless-controller wtp-profile`, `edit FAP231F-office`, `config platform`, `set type 231F`), then in `config radio-2` try `set band ?` and `set channel-bonding ?` to see which values the platform allows.
3. With real hardware: set radio 2 to a DFS channel and watch the AP's channel in the Managed FortiAPs list; on the AP, `cw_diag -c all-countries` shows the regulatory table.

## Key takeaways

* Wi-Fi 4 = 802.11n, Wi-Fi 5 = 802.11ac (5 GHz only), Wi-Fi 6 = 802.11ax, Wi-Fi 6E = 802.11ax in 6 GHz, Wi-Fi 7 = 802.11be (320 MHz in 6 GHz, MLO, 4096-QAM).
* 2.4 GHz: use 1/6/11 at 20 MHz. 5 GHz: UNII-2A and UNII-2C need DFS; UNII-4 (169/173/177) only on G-series in Single 5G mode and only where legal.
* 6 GHz allows only WPA3 or OWE with PMF, and SAE must use H2E.
* DFS: listen before use, vacate on radar, stay away 30 minutes. FortiOS `zero-wait-dfs` defaults to enable.
* The controller defaults to the US; set `country` under `wireless-controller setting` before creating FortiAP profiles.
* FortiOS radio power is EIRP: percentage (default 100%) or dBm (default 27). SNR = signal − noise.
