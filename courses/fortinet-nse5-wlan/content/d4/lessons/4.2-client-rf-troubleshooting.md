## A layered approach

Not every Wi-Fi complaint is a radio problem. Fortinet's best-practice guidance sorts the usual causes by OSI layer; working bottom-up stops you from tuning channels to fix a DHCP scope.

| Layer | Common causes | First tools |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Physical (RF) | Weak signal, low client capability (802.11b, 1x1), co-channel and adjacent-channel Wi-Fi interference, non-Wi-Fi noise (microwaves, Bluetooth, cordless phones, medical equipment) | Signal/SNR in WiFi Clients, `wlac -c ap-rogue`, spectrum analysis, site survey |
| 2 Data link (802.11 MAC) | Too many clients per channel (contention and backoff), too many high-priority WMM clients, wrong passphrase or security settings, too many beacons from too many SSIDs | `sta_filter` debug, event logs, client counts |
| 3 and up | DHCP, DNS, too much broadcast/multicast, unwanted applications | DHCP leases, extended logs, FortiView, firewall policies, application control, traffic shaping |

## Client connection problems

Fortinet groups the symptoms like this. Learn the symptom → first suspect mapping.

| Symptom | Check |
|---|---|
| **Cannot connect at all** | Security and authentication settings on the client match the SSID (mode, passphrase, EAP method, certificates); update client drivers and firmware; if other clients connect fine, suspect device interoperability and run debugs and a sniffer; look for rogue suppression knocking clients off; try a simpler PHY mode on the client to test interoperability |
| **Connects, then drops and reconnects** | Client sleep or power-saving behaviour (often fixed by drivers), the client flapping between APs (roaming sensitivity, a "preferred" other network), an IP conflict in DHCP |
| **Drops and never comes back** | It roamed to another SSID, standby/sleep; toggle the client's Wi-Fi interface |
| **Connects but gets no IP** | DHCP server, scope exhaustion, the VLAN/trunk path for bridged SSIDs, broadcast handling |

The tool that separates these is the **client debug** (`diagnose wireless-controller wlac sta_filter <MAC> 2` with the `cw_acd` debug enabled, lesson 4.3). It shows the association, the 4-way handshake and DHCP, so you can see whether the client:

* never associates (wrong SSID settings, weak signal, load balancing or probe suppression ignoring it),
* associates but fails the handshake (wrong PSK: message 2/4 arrives but 3/4 is never sent; extended logs say "invalid MIC in 2/4 message"),
* fails 802.1X (RADIUS reject or timeout: `RADIUS-auth-failure` logs; check `auth-timeout` and the RADIUS server's own logs),
* completes the handshake but never gets a DHCP ACK.

For PSK SSIDs, an administrator can read the configured passphrase in plain text from `config wireless-controller vap` to compare it with what users type.

## RF problems

### Weak signal and asymmetric power

APs often transmit far louder than battery-powered clients. A phone can hear the AP at -65 dBm while the AP hears the phone at -80 dBm, so the link fails upstream even though the client shows full bars. Fortinet's guidance: measure signal in **both directions** and match AP power to the weakest clients it must serve (roughly 10 dBm for phones and 14 dBm for laptops in Fortinet's examples).

* Configured power: FortiAP profile radio `power-mode` (`percentage` default or `dBm`), `power-level`/`power-value`, or **automatic Tx power** (`auto-power-level`, `auto-power-high`, `auto-power-low`, `auto-power-target`).
* Actual power on the AP: `iwconfig wlan00` → `Tx-Power=`. The regulatory domain and the AP model can cap what you configured.
* Client view: the Signal Strength widget, WiFi Clients columns, or client tools (`netsh wlan show networks mode=bssid` on Windows shows percentages, not dBm).

For new or redesigned areas, a **site survey** with a scaled floor plan beats guessing.

### Interference

| Type | Source | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Co-channel / coherent** | Other Wi-Fi BSSs on the same channel, poor channel planning, cells too large | Change channel, DARRP, reduce power, more 5 GHz |
| **Non-coherent** | Non-Wi-Fi emitters: Bluetooth, microwave ovens, cordless phones, X-ray and other equipment | Find and move the source (spectrum analysis), move to 5 GHz, change channel |

In 2.4 GHz use only channels **1, 6 and 11** at 20 MHz; anything else overlaps. Prefer 5 GHz, which has many more non-overlapping 20 MHz channels. Fortinet's best practice also recommends static non-DFS UNII-1/UNII-3 channels for latency-sensitive applications, because a DFS channel must be vacated when radar is detected. Fortinet wireless adapters ignore signals of -95 dBm or weaker.

A periodic drop in an otherwise strong signal is the classic interference symptom. Spectrum analysis (Dedicated Monitor radio, **Diagnostics and Tools > Spectrum Analysis**, or `execute wireless-controller spectral-scan` + `diagnose wireless-controller wlac -c rf-sa`) shows duty cycle and power per frequency, which reveals non-Wi-Fi sources that `ap-rogue` can never show.

### Cell size and density

* Many APs at high power make big overlapping cells: clients hear several APs on the same channel, airtime is shared, and clients stay stuck to far APs. Fortinet's guidance for high capacity is **smaller cells** (lower power, for example around 10 dBm) and more roaming.
* Automatic Tx power uses neighbour scan reports: if another managed AP is heard stronger than `auto-power-target` (default -70 dBm) the radio lowers power toward `auto-power-low`; if weaker, it raises toward `auto-power-high`. Defaults 10 and 17 dBm.
* DARRP picks channels per radio (`set darrp enable`) using `arrp-profile` thresholds and weights, optimised on the `darrp-optimize` schedule (default every 86400 s inside the `default-darrp-optimize` schedule, 01:00–01:30).

## Throughput problems

Fortinet's checklist:

* **Ping from the client**: more than about 10 ms delay suggests weak client signal, an overloaded AP, interference or weak AP power.
* **Measure** with iPerf or a timed file copy rather than trusting the link rate.
* **No TKIP**: TKIP caps 802.11n clients at legacy rates; use WPA2/WPA3 with AES-CCMP.
* **Client capability**: Fortinet's examples give 130 Mbps as the maximum for a 2x2 client on 2.4 GHz and 300 Mbps for a 2x2 client on 5 GHz (802.11n with short guard interval and channel bonding).
* **CAPWAP fragmentation**: full-size packets in tunnel mode can fragment; keep `ip-fragment-preventing tcp-mss-adjust` (default) and tune `tun-mtu-uplink`/`downlink`.
* **Layer 3+**: too much broadcast/multicast and unwanted applications; use application control, web filtering and traffic shaping on the SSID's firewall policies.

## Remediation toolbox

| Problem | FortiOS setting |
|---|---|
| Distant clients wasting airtime with probes | SSID `probe-resp-suppression enable`, `probe-resp-threshold` (default -80 dBm) |
| Sticky clients that will not roam | `sticky-client-remove enable` with per-band thresholds; disable low basic rates (`rates-11a`, `rates-11bg`) |
| Legacy 802.11b slowing 2.4 GHz | Radio `powersave-optimize no-11b-rate` (frames sent at 6 Mbps or higher instead of 1 Mbps) |
| Dual-band clients on 2.4 GHz | `frequency-handoff enable` (band steering), `handoff-rssi` |
| One AP overloaded | `ap-handoff enable`, `handoff-sta-thresh`; `max-clients` on the SSID or radio |
| Broadcast/multicast load | SSID `broadcast-suppression` (default `dhcp-up arp-known`), `multicast-enhance enable` (multicast to unicast) |
| Co-channel interference | DARRP, automatic Tx power, more 5 GHz, honeycomb channel reuse |
| Fragmentation in the tunnel | `ip-fragment-preventing`, `tun-mtu-uplink`/`tun-mtu-downlink` |
| A client that must be stopped now | Quarantine or Disassociate from the WiFi Clients monitor |

> **Exam tip:** Match the fix to the layer. "Strong signal but periodic drops" → interference (change channel, spectrum analysis). "Good signal at the client but the AP barely hears it" → asymmetric power (lower AP power). "Associated but no IP" → DHCP/VLAN, not RF.

## Key takeaways

* Troubleshoot by layer: RF, then 802.11 association and security, then IP services.
* Client symptoms: cannot connect (settings, drivers, interop), drops and reconnects (power save, flapping, IP conflict), no IP (DHCP/VLAN).
* Asymmetric power: measure both directions, match AP power to the weakest client, check actual power with `iwconfig`.
* Coherent interference = other Wi-Fi; non-coherent = non-Wi-Fi sources; use 1/6/11 in 2.4 GHz and prefer 5 GHz.
* Throughput: ping < 10 ms, iPerf, no TKIP, CAPWAP MSS/MTU, layer 3+ shaping.
* Remediation settings: probe response suppression, sticky client removal, no-11b-rate, frequency/AP handoff, broadcast suppression, multicast-enhance, DARRP and automatic Tx power.
