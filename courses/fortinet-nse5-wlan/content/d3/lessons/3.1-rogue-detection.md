## What counts as a rogue

Every FortiAP that scans the air will hear dozens of other access points: the coffee shop next door, a printer's Wi-Fi Direct network, a neighbour tenant's office. None of those is a threat just because it is on the air. In Fortinet's vocabulary:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Neighbouring / interfering AP** | Any BSSID your APs hear that your controller does not manage. It uses airtime and may cause co-channel interference, but it has no path into your network. |
| **Rogue AP** | An AP your controller does not manage that is **connected to your wired network**. It is a back door: anyone who joins it lands on your LAN, past your firewall policies and your SSID security. |
| **Hotspot hacker / evil twin** | An attacker's AP that copies one of your SSIDs (often with a stronger signal) so your users associate to it and hand over credentials or traffic. It may never touch your wired network. FortiOS calls a copy of your SSID a **fake** (phishing) SSID. |

The controller cannot read intent, so it gives you data (who heard the BSSID, how strong, on which channel, with what security, and whether it appears to be on your wire) and lets you classify each entry.

## Where the scan data comes from

FortiAP radios can feed the rogue list in two ways.

### Background scanning by AP-mode radios

A radio in `mode ap` serves clients but can briefly leave its channel to listen on others. This is controlled by a **WIDS profile** applied to the radio:

```text
FGT-HQ # config wireless-controller wids-profile
FGT-HQ (wids-profile) # edit "wids-office"
FGT-HQ (wids-office) # set ap-scan enable
FGT-HQ (wids-office) # set sensor-mode both
FGT-HQ (wids-office) # end
FGT-HQ # config wireless-controller wtp-profile
FGT-HQ (wtp-profile) # edit "FAP231F-office"
FGT-HQ (FAP231F-office) # config radio-1
FGT-HQ (radio-1) # set wids-profile "wids-office"
FGT-HQ (radio-1) # end
FGT-HQ (FAP231F-office) # end
```

* `ap-scan` is the master switch for rogue AP detection in the WIDS profile (default **disable**).
* `sensor-mode` decides what the radio monitors: `disable` (default), `foreign` (all channels except the one it is serving on) or `both` (foreign plus the home channel). In the GUI (WiFi & Switch Controller > WIDS Profiles) these appear as **Foreign Channels Only** and **Foreign and Home Channels**; choosing a sensor mode and enabling rogue AP detection also turns on on-wire detection.
* The `ap-bgscan-*` settings tune the background scan: how often a scan cycle starts (`ap-bgscan-period`), the gap between channels (`ap-bgscan-intv`), the dwell time per channel (`ap-bgscan-duration`), how long the channel must be idle before the radio leaves it (`ap-bgscan-idle`) and how often results are reported (`ap-bgscan-report-intv`). `ap-bgscan-disable-schedules` turns background scanning off during firewall schedules you choose, for example business hours on a busy voice SSID.

> **Watch out:** Fortinet's own pages disagree on the background-scan defaults. The FortiAP 7.6 guide describes a scan cycle every 300 seconds with 20 ms on each channel, but the FortiOS 7.6.3 CLI Reference lists `ap-bgscan-period` 600, `ap-bgscan-intv` 3, `ap-bgscan-duration` 30 and `ap-bgscan-idle` 20. Know what each knob does; check `show full-configuration` on your own build for the numbers.

Background scanning trades a little service quality for visibility: while the radio is off-channel it cannot serve its clients. `ap-bgscan-idle` reduces the risk of lost frames on busy radios at the cost of scanning less often.

### Dedicated monitor radios

A radio in `mode monitor` (GUI: **Dedicated Monitor**) serves no clients and scans full time. Many FortiAP models have a third radio for exactly this. On FortiAP F-series profiles (the case Fortinet's guide documents) the platform option `ddscan` (dedicated scan) uses one radio for full-band scanning, and it is enabled automatically on new F-series profiles. Disabling `ddscan` frees that radio and makes the WIDS profile option available on radios 1 and 2 for background scanning instead.

```text
FGT-HQ # config wireless-controller wtp-profile
FGT-HQ (wtp-profile) # edit "FAP431G-hd"
FGT-HQ (FAP431G-hd) # config radio-3
FGT-HQ (radio-3) # set mode monitor
FGT-HQ (radio-3) # set wids-profile "wids-office"
FGT-HQ (radio-3) # end
```

Three more WIDS-profile options shape what is reported:

* `ap-scan-threshold` (default `-90` dBm): an AP weaker than this is not reported, which keeps distant noise out of the list.
* `ap-scan-channel-list-2G-5G` and `ap-scan-channel-list-6G`: limit the foreground scan to the channels you care about so each cycle is shorter and reports arrive more often.
* `ap-scan-passive`: when enabled the radio never sends probe requests while scanning; when disabled it scans passively only on DFS channels.

The radio `mode` values you must know for the exam:

| `mode` | Role |
|---|---|
| `ap` (default) | Serves clients; can background scan with a WIDS profile |
| `monitor` | Dedicated monitor: scans for APs and stations, feeds the rogue list and WIDS |
| `sniffer` | Captures 802.11 frames on one channel to a pcap on the AP |
| `sam` | Service Assurance Management: acts as a client of another AP and runs ping or iperf tests |
| `disabled` | Radio off |

## On-wire detection

Hearing a BSSID tells you nothing about whether it is plugged into your LAN. The FortiGate answers that by correlating two tables: the MAC addresses of wireless stations its APs see on the air (and which BSSID each one talks to) and the MAC addresses it sees on its own wired interfaces. Two methods run at the same time:

| Method | How it matches | Catches |
|---|---|---|
| **Exact MAC address match** | A client MAC seen on the air through an unknown BSSID also shows up on the wired LAN | A rogue that bridges (no NAT), for example a consumer AP in bridge mode plugged into a wall jack |
| **MAC adjacency** | The rogue's wireless MAC is numerically close to a MAC seen on the LAN (its own wired/WAN MAC) | A rogue that is a NAT router: client MACs never reach your LAN, but the router's wired MAC does, and vendors number the radio and Ethernet MACs close together |

The adjacency window is set globally:

```text
FGT-HQ # config wireless-controller global
FGT-HQ (global) # set rogue-scan-mac-adjacency 7
FGT-HQ (global) # end
```

The range is 0–31 and the default is **7**.

Limitations to remember: on-wire detection needs **at least one client associated to the suspect AP and actively passing traffic**, and MAC adjacency only works when the router's wireless and wired MACs really are close. A rogue with no clients, or one whose MACs are far apart, will not be flagged on-wire.

## The rogue AP monitor

Go to **Dashboard > WiFi > Rogue APs**. Three charts sit on top (Interfering FortiAPs, SSID, Vendor Info) and a table lists every detected AP with MAC address, SSID, state, signal, vendor, which AP detected it, channel, on-wire status, security type, first seen, last seen and rate. **Show Offline** and **Show Accepted** toggle hidden entries.

The **State** column is your classification:

| State | Use it for |
|---|---|
| **Unclassified** | Initial state of every newly discovered AP. You can return an entry here if you classified it by mistake. |
| **Accepted** | Authorised APs, or neighbours you have reviewed and judged harmless. Hidden unless you click Show Accepted. |
| **Rogue** | Unauthorised APs that on-wire detection shows are attached to your network. |
| **Suppressed Rogue AP** | Rogue APs you want the controller to actively attack (see the next lesson). |

In the **On-wire** column a green up-arrow marks a suspected on-wire rogue and a red down-arrow marks an AP that is not suspected. Classification never happens on its own: on-wire detection flags, you decide.

The CLI view is `diagnose wireless-controller wlac -c ap-rogue`. Each detected BSSID prints on a summary line followed by one line per FortiAP that heard it. The first column is a four-letter code with this legend:

```text
C - Configured  (G:accept, B:rogue, S:suppress, U:unconfigured)
M - AC managed  (V:vdom, C:AC, N:unmanaged)
W - On wire     (Y:yes, N:no)
P - Phishing    (F:fake, O:offending, N:no)
```

So `UNYN` reads: unconfigured (not yet classified), not managed by this controller, on wire, not a phishing SSID. That entry deserves attention. `UNNN` is an ordinary neighbour. `BNYN` is one you have already marked Rogue.

The `ap-status` table stores your decisions. The GUI writes to it, and you can add entries yourself:

```text
FGT-HQ # config wireless-controller ap-status
FGT-HQ (ap-status) # edit 1
FGT-HQ (1) # set bssid 9c:3d:cf:5a:11:20
FGT-HQ (1) # set ssid "Linksys-Lab"
FGT-HQ (1) # set status rogue
FGT-HQ (1) # end
```

`status` accepts `rogue` (the default), `accepted` or `suppressed`.

## Logging and memory

Rogue detections are logged as wireless events and forwarded to FortiAnalyzer if you log there. By default a **rogue AP generates an alert-level log** and an **unknown AP a warning-level log**, which helps with PCI DSS evidence. `config wireless-controller timers` → `rogue-ap-log` can add periodic logging of rogue APs (default 0, off).

Busy sites can detect thousands of BSSIDs. `diagnose wireless-controller wlac -c stats` shows how many rogue APs, rogue stations and station-capability entries the controller holds and how much memory they use. To cap it, `config wireless-controller global` has `max-rogue-ap`, `max-rogue-sta` and `max-sta-cap` (0 = no limit) plus `max-rogue-ap-wtp` (how many detecting-AP records are kept per rogue, 1–16, default 16), and `config wireless-controller timers` has `rogue-ap-cleanup` and `rogue-sta-cleanup`, the minutes to keep an entry after it disappears (0 = keep).

> **Exam tip:** If a question asks why an obviously plugged-in rogue router is *not* flagged on-wire, look for "no clients connected to it" or "its wireless and wired MAC addresses are far apart". Both defeat on-wire detection.

> **Real world:** Exempting an AP from scanning (assign it a WIDS profile with rogue detection off) is allowed, but PCI DSS environments are expected to scan everywhere. Document any exemption.

## Key takeaways

* A rogue is an unmanaged AP **on your wired network**; a neighbour is just on the air; an evil twin copies your SSID.
* Scan data comes from background scanning on AP-mode radios (WIDS profile with `ap-scan enable`) or from a dedicated monitor radio (`mode monitor`, or `ddscan` on F-series profiles).
* `sensor-mode` is `disable`, `foreign` or `both`; `ap-scan-threshold` (default -90 dBm) filters weak APs.
* On-wire detection uses **exact MAC match** (bridged rogues) and **MAC adjacency** (NAT routers, `rogue-scan-mac-adjacency` default 7) and needs an active client on the rogue.
* States: Unclassified (initial), Accepted, Rogue, Suppressed Rogue AP; you classify, the controller does not.
* `wlac -c ap-rogue` codes: C (G/B/S/U), M (V/C/N), W (Y/N), P (F/O/N).
* Rogue APs log at alert level, unknown APs at warning level.
