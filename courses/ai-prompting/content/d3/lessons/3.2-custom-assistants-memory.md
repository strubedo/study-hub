## Reusable assistants

Typing the same role, rules and reference material into every new chat gets old quickly. All three vendors offer a way to save standing instructions plus knowledge files into a reusable workspace or assistant. They are the app-level version of the system prompt from Lesson 2.2 plus the file retrieval from Lesson 3.1.

| Vendor | Feature | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Anthropic | **Projects** (in the Claude app) | A self-contained workspace with its own chat history, uploaded knowledge and project instructions |
| Google | **Gems** (in the Gemini app) | A saved custom version of Gemini with your instructions and optional files |
| OpenAI | **Custom GPTs** (in ChatGPT) | A saved custom version of ChatGPT with instructions and knowledge files (details unverified, see below) |

> **Real world:** Product names, plan limits and sharing rules change quickly. Everything below is dated "as of October 2026" and comes from the vendors' own pages where it could be fetched. For ChatGPT custom GPTs and ChatGPT memory, OpenAI's help-center pages could not be fetched when this course was written, so treat the details of those two features as unverified and check help.openai.com yourself.

## Claude Projects

From Anthropic's support page (as of October 2026):

- A project is a workspace with its **own chat histories and knowledge base**. You upload documents, provide context and hold focused chats inside it.
- **Project instructions** tailor Claude's behaviour for every chat in the project, for example a formal tone or a specific role.
- Projects are available on all plans including free accounts, with a cap of five projects on free accounts.
- On paid plans, when project knowledge approaches the context limit, Claude switches to **retrieval** (RAG, Lesson 3.1) to hold more content than fits in a window.
- On Team and Enterprise plans, projects can be **shared** with specific people or the whole organization, with "can view" or "can edit" permissions, subject to admin controls.
- Anthropic also notes a **new version of projects (beta)** rolling out in stages, starting with Claude Code, in which a project is a single conversation that fans work out to parallel cloud threads with the project's files, instructions and memory. The page says the rest of its text describes the current version. This is a good example of why you verify before relying on a feature description.

## Gemini Gems

From Google's help page (as of October 2026):

- A Gem is a customized Gemini you create and save for a repeated task. You give it **instructions** and can add **knowledge files**, either uploaded from your device or from Google Drive.
- Gems can be **shared**, and Gems shared with you are not automatically added to your own Gem manager.
- Google's page warns that, **starting in November 2026, Gems will go away on personal Google accounts and automatically transition to skills.** Work or school accounts may be subject to different terms, so check which applies to you.
- Learning point: a feature you build a habit around can be renamed, merged or retired. Keep your instructions in a text file you own so you can recreate them anywhere.

## Custom GPTs

Custom GPTs are OpenAI's saved assistants, built on the same idea: a name, instructions and uploaded knowledge. OpenAI's help-center page that defines the current feature set could not be fetched. The exact current capabilities, sharing options and plan requirements are **unverified here** (the help center blocked automated access), so do not rely on any detail beyond the general concept without checking.

## What goes into a good custom assistant

Treat the instructions like a system prompt (Lesson 2.2), and keep the knowledge files like a small RAG corpus (Lesson 3.1).

```text
Role: You are a network change reviewer for a small enterprise.

Always:
- Review the supplied change plan against the attached standards.
- Reply with a table: step | risk | missing verification | fix.
- Cite the standard's section number for each finding.
- If the standards do not cover something, say "No standard
  applies" instead of guessing.

Never:
- Invent commands. If unsure of syntax for a platform, say so.
- Ask for passwords or keys; tell the user to redact them.

Knowledge files: change-standard.md, rollback-template.md
```

Design tips:

1. **One job per assistant.** A "change reviewer" and a "log summarizer" are two assistants. Broad assistants get vague.
2. **Put rules in instructions and reference material in files.** Rules you need every time belong in instructions; long reference text belongs in files the assistant can search.
3. **Name your files clearly and keep them focused.** Several short, titled files beat one 300-page dump when retrieval is involved.
4. **Define the output format** and what to say when the files have no answer.
5. **Keep a copy of the instructions** outside the product, with a version date.
6. **Test with a fixed set of questions**, including ones whose answer is not in the files, every time you change the instructions or files.
7. **Do not store secrets** in instructions or files. Anyone who can use or share the assistant might extract them, and instructions are not a security boundary.

> **Exam tip:** Instructions and knowledge files in a custom assistant influence the model; they do not retrain it. The model's underlying knowledge and its tendency to hallucinate are unchanged, so still ask for citations and verify.

## Memory and personalization

Chats are stateless by default: a new conversation starts with no knowledge of the old one (Lesson 1.1). **Memory** features add text from earlier conversations to the context of later ones. Three flavours:

1. **Standing preferences.** You write "I am a CCNA-level engineer; give Cisco IOS-XE examples" once in a settings box, and the app adds it to every chat (the "custom instructions" idea from Lesson 2.2).
2. **Saved memories.** The assistant itself notes facts about you or your projects from conversations and recalls them later.
3. **Search of past chats.** The assistant can look up earlier conversations on request, using retrieval.

What the vendors say (as of October 2026):

- **Claude.** Anthropic's support page says Claude can search previous conversations (a paid-plan feature, using RAG, visible as tool calls in the chat) and can generate **memory** from your chats to carry context into new conversations. Searches cover chats outside projects and, within a project, only that project's chats. You can switch the features off in settings, and **incognito chats** are not saved to history and are not pulled into later searches. The page lists memory as on by default for Free, Pro, Max and Ultra plans; Team and Enterprise behaviour differs and admin policy applies.
- **Gemini.** Google's page lists personalization based on the memory of your past Gemini chats, content and activity from Google apps you connect, and your own instructions. It says these features are not available to everyone, require a personal Google Account, and are not available with work, school or supervised accounts.
- **ChatGPT.** Memory and custom instructions exist, but the help-center pages could not be fetched, so treat the details as unverified.

### Memory trade-offs

| Benefit | Risk |
|---|---|
| Less repetition; consistent tone and examples | Stale or wrong memories quietly shape answers |
| Continuity across sessions | Sensitive details persist where you may not expect |
| Better-fitting suggestions | Different results from the same prompt, which complicates testing |
| Convenient for personal use | Policies for work accounts, retention and training differ; check before using with company data |

Practical habits: review and prune what is stored, use incognito or temporary chats for sensitive or experimental work, and when you test a prompt for repeatability, turn memory off so it is the prompt that you are testing.

> **Watch out:** Memory is added text, not learning. The model's weights do not change because you chat with it. If a memory says "customer prefers OSPF" and the next project uses EIGRP, the assistant may still push OSPF until you correct or delete the memory.

## Try it yourself

Use two assistants from different vendors (for example Claude and Gemini; if you have ChatGPT, use a custom GPT or a project there).

1. **Build the change reviewer.** Write a short invented change standard (10 rules) as a text file. Create a Project, Gem (before it is migrated) or custom GPT with the instructions above and the file. Give it a fake change plan that breaks rules 3 and 7. Did it cite the right sections?
2. **Test the edge.** Ask something the standard does not cover, such as "Can I use telnet in the lab?" if your standard is silent. Does it say none applies, or invent a rule?
3. **Memory check.** In an assistant with memory on, say "For this conversation, assume all our switches are Catalyst 9300 running IOS-XE." Start a new chat and ask for a VLAN config without naming the platform. Look at the settings page to see what, if anything, was stored. Delete it.
4. **Repeatability.** Run the same prompt with memory on and off. Do the answers differ in a way that would affect your testing?

## Key takeaways

- Projects, Gems and custom GPTs bundle standing instructions and knowledge files into a reusable assistant.
- Instructions work like a system prompt; knowledge files work like a small retrieval corpus; neither retrains the model.
- Claude Projects: own knowledge and chats, paid plans use RAG for large knowledge, sharing on Team and Enterprise.
- Gems: instructions plus files, and Google says Gems on personal accounts transition to skills starting November 2026.
- ChatGPT custom GPT and memory details were not verifiable against the help center here; verify before use.
- Memory adds saved text or searches past chats; it is stateful context, not learning, and can go stale.
- Review and prune memory, use incognito or temporary chats for sensitive work, and keep a copy of your instructions.
- Never put secrets in instructions or knowledge files.
