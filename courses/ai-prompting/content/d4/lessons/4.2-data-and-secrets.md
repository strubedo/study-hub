## Know where your prompt goes

Everything you type or upload is sent to the vendor's servers and handled under that vendor's terms for your **kind of account**. The same assistant can treat the same text differently on a free personal account and on a company workspace. This lesson teaches the concepts and the habit of checking, because the details change often. Specifics below are as of October 2026; confirm them on the vendor's own pages before relying on them.

## Four account types, four sets of terms

| Account type | Typical examples | Default posture on training (concept) | Who controls settings |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Consumer** | Free or individual paid plans of ChatGPT, Claude or the Gemini app on a personal Google account | Depends on a user-level setting; chats may be used to improve models unless you opt out or use a temporary or incognito mode, and some content can be reviewed by people or kept for safety review | You |
| **Team / business** | Team and business workspaces, Google Workspace with Gemini | Vendors state that workspace content is not used to train models by default | Workspace admin plus user |
| **Enterprise** | Enterprise tiers with admin controls, SSO, audit, sometimes custom retention | Not used for training by default; stronger admin, retention and compliance controls | Organisation admins and contract |
| **API** | Anthropic API, OpenAI API, Gemini API paid tier | Developer data is generally not used for training by default under commercial terms; abuse-monitoring logs may still be kept for a limited time | Developer, per project and contract |

What the vendor pages actually say (checked October 2026):

- **Anthropic.** Its Privacy Center distinguishes consumer products (Free, Pro, Max, and Claude Code used with those accounts) from commercial products (Claude for Work and the Anthropic API). For consumer plans, chats and coding sessions are used to improve models if you allow it through a Model Improvement setting, or in cases such as safety review; Incognito chats are not used for improvement. For commercial products, the stated default is that inputs and outputs are not used to train models, subject to the commercial terms.
- **OpenAI.** The API documentation states that data sent to the API is not used to train or improve OpenAI models unless you explicitly opt in, and describes abuse-monitoring logs that are retained for a limited time by default, with Zero Data Retention available to eligible customers. The consumer ChatGPT data controls and the business and enterprise privacy pages sit on pages that did not load for automated checking, so treat the exact consumer and business statements as **unverified here**; read the "Data controls" settings in the app and the vendor's privacy pages.
- **Google.** The Gemini Apps Privacy Hub explains Gemini Apps Activity: with it on, activity can be used to improve Google services and some chats are reviewed by human reviewers. Reviewed chats are kept separately from your activity, so deleting your activity does not delete them. With activity off or in temporary chats, Google still processes chats to respond to you and for safety. Google's Workspace documentation states that prompts, content and responses in Gemini for Workspace are not used to train generative AI models without permission. The Gemini API has separate terms for paid and unpaid services; read which tier you use.

> **Watch out:** A personal account used for work is the usual mistake. You might be logged into a consumer account in a browser profile while believing you are on the company workspace. Check the account name and plan before pasting anything.

Neither a setting nor a plan makes a paste safe by itself. Data may still be stored for a period, visible to admins, or exposed if the account is compromised. A training opt-out is about model training. It is not an encryption or retention guarantee.

## Settings to check before first use

- **Training / "improve the model" toggle.** Find it, read what it controls, and set it deliberately.
- **Chat history and temporary modes.** ChatGPT's temporary chat, Claude's incognito chat and Gemini's Keep Activity setting change what is saved. Understand what they do not change (for example, safety review or abuse-monitoring).
- **Memory and saved context.** Memory features carry information from one chat into later ones. Review what is stored; do not let secrets become permanent context.
- **Connected apps and files.** Connectors (mail, drive, repos) give the assistant access to content you did not paste. They widen both the benefit and the exposure.
- **Sharing links.** A shared conversation link can expose everything in the chat to anyone with the link, depending on the product settings.
- **Company policy.** Your employer may require an approved tool. Following the policy matters more than any individual setting.

## What must never be pasted

Treat any chat prompt as a place where text can be logged, reviewed or leaked. Never paste:

| Category | Examples | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Credentials** | Passwords, enable secrets, SNMP community strings, API keys, SSH private keys, bearer tokens, Wi-Fi PSKs | Direct access to systems |
| **Hashed or encoded secrets** | Cisco type 5, 8 and 9 hashes, type 7 strings, base64 blobs | Type 7 is reversible; hashes can be cracked |
| **VPN material** | IPsec pre-shared keys, certificates and private keys, WireGuard keys | Authenticate tunnels |
| **Personal data** | Customer names, email addresses, ticket contents with PII | Privacy law and contract |
| **Unreleased or confidential material** | Customer topologies, incident reports not yet public, proprietary code | Contract and competitive harm |
| **Internal identifiers that map your network** | Real public IP ranges, hostnames, VLAN schemes and admin usernames | Reconnaissance value |

In a Cisco config, hunt for `enable secret`, `enable password`, `username ... secret`, `snmp-server community`, `tacacs-server key`, `radius-server key`, `crypto isakmp key`, and `key-string`. FortiOS has `set psksecret`, `set password` and `set passwd` lines (and `ENC` strings), and PAN-OS config includes phash values and keys. When in doubt, remove the entire line.

> **Real world:** If a secret did get pasted, treat it as exposed. Rotate it. Deleting the chat does not undo the transmission.

## Redact before you paste

A good redaction keeps the *structure* the model needs and removes the *values* that are sensitive.

1. **Replace secrets with labelled placeholders**, not blanks: `<SNMP-COMMUNITY>`, `<PSK-SITE-B>`. The model can still reason about where they appear.
2. **Replace real addresses consistently** with documentation ranges (RFC 5737: 192.0.2.0/24, 198.51.100.0/24, 203.0.113.0/24) and replace hostnames with generic ones. Keep the same mapping so relationships survive (the same real address always becomes the same placeholder).
3. **Remove entire sections** the question does not need (users, AAA, certificates).
4. **Automate it.** A short script that applies a search-and-replace list is faster and less error-prone than editing by hand, and you can test it on a sample. Review the output before pasting.
5. **Reverse the mapping** yourself when you read the answer.

```text
Before:  snmp-server community Winter2026-RO RO 10
After:   snmp-server community <SNMP-RO-COMMUNITY> RO 10
```

Ask a model to redact only when the tool itself is approved for that data; otherwise you have just sent the secret to the model to ask it to hide the secret.

## A simple decision

```text
Is the assistant approved for this data class by my employer?  No -> stop.
Does the text contain secrets or personal data?               Yes -> remove.
Does the model need the real values to do the job?            Usually no -> placeholders.
Is it a work account with the right plan, history and settings? Check, then paste.
```

## Try it yourself

1. Open the data settings of two assistants you use. Write down, for each: is there a training toggle, what does temporary mode change, where is history deleted, and is memory on? Mark anything you cannot find.
2. Take a sanitized sample router config (use a lab device) and write a list of regex rules that mask secrets, community strings and public addresses. Run it, then read the result line by line to confirm nothing leaked.
3. Ask an assistant to review the redacted config and check whether its advice still makes sense with placeholders.
4. Look up the official privacy page for each vendor you use and note the date you checked.

## Key takeaways

- Data handling depends on the **account type**: consumer, team/business, enterprise and API terms differ.
- Training settings, history and memory are separate controls; learn which does what and check them before use.
- Vendors' commercial terms generally state that business and API content is not used for model training by default; consumer plans often depend on a setting. Verify on the vendor's page; details change.
- A training opt-out does not mean no storage, no review for safety or abuse, or no risk if an account is compromised.
- Never paste credentials, keys, hashes, tokens or personal data; if one escapes, rotate it.
- Redact with consistent placeholders so the structure survives, and automate and check the redaction.
- Follow your employer's policy and use the approved tool and account.
