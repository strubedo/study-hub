## Why interoperation matters

No campus becomes an SD-Access fabric overnight. The fabric has to reach the data center, the WAN, the internet and the shared services (DHCP, DNS, ISE, Catalyst Center itself), and during migration it must talk to the buildings that are still traditional Layer 2 access with distribution-layer gateways. Topic 1.3.b asks how that works. Everything below happens at the **border node**, plus a few techniques for carrying group tags outside the fabric.

## Layer 3 handoff: border to fusion device

The common pattern for connecting a fabric to the outside world is an **IP-based transit** from the border node to a **fusion device** (a router, Layer 3 switch or firewall outside the fabric):

1. Each fabric VN is a VRF on the border. For each VN, the border uses an 802.1Q sub-interface or a VLAN with an SVI on the link toward the fusion device (**VRF-lite**).
2. The border runs **eBGP** per VRF with the fusion device and advertises the fabric's subnets (the anycast gateway pools) out of each VN.
3. The fusion device either keeps the VRFs separate (toward a firewall that inspects inter-VN traffic) or **leaks routes** between the VN VRFs and the global table or a shared-services VRF, so every VN can reach DHCP, DNS and ISE.
4. The border imports the external routes (the data center, shared services, a default route) back into the fabric and registers them, so edge nodes can reach them through the border.

Catalyst Center automates the border side: for every VN on a Layer 3 handoff it needs a VLAN for the SVI that peers with the external device and an address pair (or a pool to allocate them from). The fusion device side is usually configured manually.

```text
! Fusion device side of one VN (CAMPUS) on an IP transit
vrf definition CAMPUS
 rd 65001:10
 address-family ipv4
 exit-address-family
!
interface GigabitEthernet1/0/1.3001
 encapsulation dot1Q 3001
 vrf forwarding CAMPUS
 ip address 172.16.255.2 255.255.255.252
!
router bgp 65002
 address-family ipv4 vrf CAMPUS
  neighbor 172.16.255.1 remote-as 65001
  neighbor 172.16.255.1 activate
```

Here the border uses AS 65001 and the fusion device AS 65002 (private ASNs); repeat the sub-interface and BGP neighbor per VN.

> **Exam tip:** Segmentation survives the handoff because each VN has its own VRF and BGP session. If the fusion device leaks everything into one table, the macro-segmentation between VNs is gone at that point, by design or by mistake.

## Internal, external and default exit

| Border type | Imports | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal border** | Specific known prefixes (shared services, data center) into the fabric | Reaching known internal networks |
| **External border** | Nothing specific; acts as the **default exit** (gateway of last resort) for unknown destinations | Internet and WAN |
| **Internal and external** (anywhere) | Both | Small sites with one border pair |

Catalyst Center models these with a "default exit" setting (make this border the gateway of last resort) and an "import external routes" setting on the border.

## Layer 2 handoff: extending a fabric subnet to a traditional VLAN

For migration, a **Layer 2 border** bridges a fabric subnet onto a VLAN on a traditional switch. Hosts on the traditional VLAN and hosts on the fabric subnet are then in the same broadcast domain and use the **fabric anycast gateway**, so a building can be moved switch by switch without renumbering.

- The Layer 2 handoff maps an **internal** (fabric) VLAN to an **external** VLAN on a specific border interface; the two VLAN IDs can differ.
- The old gateway (the SVI and FHRP on the traditional distribution switch) must be shut down for that subnet, or there will be two gateways for the same subnet.
- Endpoints behind the Layer 2 border are learned by the border and registered with the control plane node like any other endpoint.
- Only one Layer 2 handoff should exist per VLAN to avoid a Layer 2 loop between the fabric and the traditional network.

> **Watch out:** A Layer 2 handoff is a migration tool. Leaving large legacy VLANs bridged into the fabric for years brings back the broadcast and STP problems the fabric removed.

## Migration approaches

| Approach | How | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **Parallel install** | Build a new fabric next to the old network, connect them at the border, move users building by building | Cleanest, needs space, power and budget for new switches |
| **Incremental (in place)** | Convert existing Catalyst 9000 switches to fabric roles one area at a time, using Layer 2 handoff for subnets that span old and new | Reuses hardware, more planning and maintenance windows |

Either way, the border connects the two worlds, and shared services must be reachable from both during the transition.

## Carrying group tags outside the fabric

Inside the fabric the SGT travels in the VXLAN Group Policy ID. Outside, there are two ways to keep the group information:

| Method | How | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Inline tagging** | The SGT rides in a Cisco Meta Data (CMD) field in the Ethernet frame on links where both ends support it | Border to a TrustSec-capable fusion switch, router or firewall |
| **SXP** (SGT Exchange Protocol) | A TCP-based control protocol sends IP-to-SGT bindings to devices that cannot tag inline in hardware | Legacy switches, firewalls that enforce by group |

ISE can also publish IP-to-SGT bindings to other systems through pxGrid. A firewall that receives the bindings can write rules by group, so policy written for the fabric extends to the data center edge.

## Traditional switches as extended nodes

Switches that cannot be fabric edges (compact, industrial, older models) can connect to a fabric edge as **extended nodes** or **policy extended nodes**. Catalyst Center onboards them and their access ports are mapped to fabric VNs; the edge node does the fabric work on their behalf. A policy extended node can also enforce SGACLs itself.

## SD-Access transit between sites

Between fabric sites, an **SD-Access transit** keeps fabric information (VN in the VNI, SGT in the Group Policy ID) end to end instead of dropping it at an IP handoff, using transit control plane nodes. Catalyst Center offers it as LISP publish/subscribe or LISP/BGP transit types, alongside the IP-based transit. An IP-based transit is the one to use toward anything that is not SD-Access (traditional sites, the WAN, SD-WAN).

## Lab it yourself

With CML, GNS3 or EVE-NG (Windows, Linux or an Intel Mac; on an Apple silicon Mac use a remote x86 server or a Cisco DevNet sandbox) and two Catalyst 8000V (or Catalyst 9000v) nodes `R1` (acting as the border) and `R2` (the fusion device):

1. Create VRFs `CAMPUS` and `IOT` on both. Link them with one physical link and two dot1Q sub-interfaces (VLAN 3001 and 3002) with /30s from `172.16.255.0/24`.
2. Run eBGP per VRF (AS 65001 on `R1`, AS 65002 on `R2`). On `R1` advertise a loopback in each VRF (`10.10.10.1/32` in CAMPUS, `10.20.10.1/32` in IOT).
3. On `R2` create a `SHARED` VRF with a loopback `10.99.99.1/32` (the "DHCP server") and leak routes between `SHARED` and each VN with route-target import and export (give each VRF an `rd` and `route-target export`/`import` lines, and add `address-family ipv4 vrf SHARED` with `redistribute connected` under `router bgp 65002` so BGP carries the loopback).
4. Verify with `show ip bgp vpnv4 all summary`, `show ip route vrf CAMPUS` and `show ip route vrf IOT`: each VN reaches SHARED, but CAMPUS and IOT cannot reach each other.

## Key takeaways

- The border node is where SD-Access meets everything else.
- Layer 3 handoff (IP transit): one VRF-lite sub-interface or SVI and one eBGP session per VN to a fusion device, which leaks routes to shared services or hands inter-VN traffic to a firewall.
- Internal borders import known prefixes; external borders are the default exit; small sites often combine both.
- Layer 2 handoff bridges a fabric subnet onto a traditional VLAN for migration; the old gateway must be removed and there must be no second Layer 2 path.
- SGTs leave the fabric by inline tagging or SXP; SD-Access transit keeps VN and SGT between fabric sites, IP transit does not.
- Migrate in parallel or incrementally; extended nodes bring non-fabric switches under fabric policy.
