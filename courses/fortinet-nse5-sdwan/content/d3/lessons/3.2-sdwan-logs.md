## Two kinds of evidence

Logs answer the question the live views can't: *what happened, and when?* For SD-WAN you combine two log families:

| Log | Tells you | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Event logs** (`type="event"`) | Member health changes, SLA pass/fail, rule member reordering, volume limits, routes withdrawn by a health check | Log & Report > **System Events**, card/subtype **SD-WAN Events** (always available) and General System Events |
| **Traffic logs** (`type="traffic"`, `subtype="forward"`) | Which rule a session matched, the rule's member quality at that moment, the egress interface | Log & Report > Forward Traffic |

Security (UTM) logs, such as IPS, web filter or application control, carry the same `policyid`, `srcintf` and `dstintf` fields, so you can tie a security event to the SD-WAN path it took.

In the CLI, event logs from the current log device can be listed with:

```text
FGT-Branch1 # execute log filter category 1
FGT-Branch1 # execute log filter field subtype sdwan
FGT-Branch1 # execute log display
```

## The SD-WAN event log IDs

| logid | logdesc | subtype | Triggered when |
|---|---|---|---|
| `0100022921` | Routing information changed | **system** | A health check declares a member down or up and the member's static routes may be removed or re-added (`update-static-route`) |
| `0113022923` | SDWAN status | sdwan | A member goes in or out of SLA, the number of members passing an SLA changes, a rule's member order changes, a rule stops or starts using a member, or a load-balancing rule's member set changes |
| `0113022924` | SDWAN volume status | sdwan | A member goes over or back under its configured session or volume ratio |
| `0113022925` | SDWAN SLA information | sdwan | Periodically, per `sla-fail-log-period` (while SLA fails) and `sla-pass-log-period` (while SLA passes) |

> **Watch out:** `0100022921` has `subtype="system"`, not `sdwan`. When you filter on SD-WAN events only, the "member dead, route removed" message isn't in the list. It's `level="critical"` and sits with the general system events.

### 0100022921: health check down or up

```text
date=2026-10-28 time=09:14:02 eventtime=1793196842104527311 tz="-0500" logid="0100022921" type="event" subtype="system" level="critical" vd="root" logdesc="Routing information changed" name="Internet_HC" interface="port2" status="down" msg="Static route on interface port2 may be removed by health-check Internet_HC. Route: (198.51.100.2->8.8.8.8 ping-down)"
```

`name` is the health check, `interface` the member, `status` `down` or `up`. The Route part shows the probe source and server and the result (`ping-down`, `ping-up`). The recovery log says "may be added".

### 0113022923: SDWAN status

This one log ID covers several events. `eventtype` tells them apart:

| eventtype | msg | Extra fields | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Health Check | `Number of pass member changed.` | `healthcheck`, `slatargetid`, `oldvalue`, `newvalue` | How many members meet SLA target N changed |
| Health Check | `Member status changed. Member out-of-sla.` / `... Member in sla.` | `healthcheck`, `slatargetid`, `member` | One member failed or passed SLA target N |
| Service | `Service prioritized by SLA will be redirected in sequence order.` | `serviceid`, `service`, `seq` | A Lowest Cost (SLA) rule reordered its members |
| Service | `Service prioritized by performance metric will be redirected in sequence order.` | `serviceid`, `service`, `metric`, `seq` | A Best Quality rule reordered its members by its link-cost factor |
| Service | `Member link is unreachable or miss threshold. Stop forwarding traffic.` / `Member link is available. Start forwarding traffic.` | `interface`, `member`, `serviceid`, `service`, `gateway` | A rule stopped or resumed using a member |
| Service | `Service will be load balanced among members with available routing.` | `serviceid`, `service`, `member` | A load-balancing rule's usable member set changed |

How to read the fields:

* `member="2"` is the member **sequence number** (`config members` / `edit 2`), not an interface index.
* `seq="2,1"` is the rule's **new member order**: member 2 is now preferred over member 1.
* `member="2(port2),1(port1)"` in a load-balancing log lists the members now sharing traffic.
* `slatargetid=1` is the SLA entry inside the health check (`config sla` / `edit 1`).

A Lowest Cost (SLA) rule losing its preferred member to an SLA failure produces a short burst like this:

```text
date=2026-10-28 time=09:20:41 eventtime=1793197241387223190 tz="-0500" logid="0113022923" type="event" subtype="sdwan" level="notice" vd="root" logdesc="SDWAN status" eventtype="Health Check" healthcheck="Internet_HC" slatargetid=1 member="1" msg="Member status changed. Member out-of-sla."
date=2026-10-28 time=09:20:41 eventtime=1793197241387238004 tz="-0500" logid="0113022923" type="event" subtype="sdwan" level="notice" vd="root" logdesc="SDWAN status" eventtype="Health Check" healthcheck="Internet_HC" slatargetid=1 oldvalue="2" newvalue="1" msg="Number of pass member changed."
date=2026-10-28 time=09:20:41 eventtime=1793197241387260115 tz="-0500" logid="0113022923" type="event" subtype="sdwan" level="notice" vd="root" logdesc="SDWAN status" eventtype="Service" serviceid=1 service="Critical-DIA" seq="2,1" msg="Service prioritized by SLA will be redirected in sequence order."
```

Read it as a sentence: member 1 failed SLA target 1 of `Internet_HC`; only one member now passes; rule 1 `Critical-DIA` now prefers member 2. Member 1 is still **alive**: there is no 0100022921 log and no "Stop forwarding traffic" log.

### 0113022924: volume status

With the Volume or Sessions implicit modes, a member that carries more than its share is put into "conservative status":

```text
logid="0113022924" type="event" subtype="sdwan" level="notice" logdesc="SDWAN volume status" eventtype="Volume" interface="port2" member="2" msg="Member enters into conservative status with limited ablity to receive new sessions for too much traffic."
```

The matching recovery says the member resumes normal status to receive new sessions. (The spelling "ablity" is FortiOS's own.)

### 0113022925: periodic SLA information

These logs are generated only if you set an interval on the health check. Both settings default to **0** (no periodic logs), range 0-3600 seconds:

```text
FGT-Branch1 # config system sdwan
FGT-Branch1 (sdwan) # config health-check
FGT-Branch1 (health-check) # edit "DC_HC"
FGT-Branch1 (DC_HC) # set sla-fail-log-period 30
FGT-Branch1 (DC_HC) # set sla-pass-log-period 300
FGT-Branch1 (DC_HC) # end
FGT-Branch1 (sdwan) # end
```

```text
date=2026-10-28 time=10:02:15 eventtime=1793199735512094012 tz="-0500" logid="0113022925" type="event" subtype="sdwan" level="notice" vd="root" logdesc="SDWAN SLA information" eventtype="SLA" healthcheck="DC_HC" slatargetid=1 interface="HUB1-VPN1" status="up" latency="38.212" jitter="2.104" packetloss="4.000%" inbandwidthavailable="0kbps" outbandwidthavailable="50.00Mbps" bibandwidthavailable="50.00Mbps" inbandwidthused="812kbps" outbandwidthused="1240kbps" bibandwidthused="2052kbps" slamap="0x0" metric="packetloss" msg="Health Check SLA status. SLA failed due to being over the performance metric threshold."
```

* `status="up"`: the member is alive.
* `slamap="0x0"`: it meets no SLA target; `metric="packetloss"` names the metric that failed.
* A pass log has `level="information"`, `slamap` with the bit set (for example `0x1`) and `msg="Health Check SLA status."`.

These periodic logs are what FortiAnalyzer uses for SD-WAN SLA reports and long-term trending. Keep the pass period long; a 1-second pass log on many health checks floods the log device.

## SD-WAN fields in traffic logs

Forward traffic logs gain SD-WAN fields when the session matched an SD-WAN rule:

| Field | Meaning (FortiOS 7.6 Log Message Reference) |
|---|---|
| `vwlid` | SD-WAN rule (service) ID |
| `vwlname` | SD-WAN rule name |
| `vwlquality` | Quality info of the rule that matched: member, state, measurement, whether selected |
| `vwlservice` | Application matched for application-based steering (`internet-service-app-ctrl`) |
| `dstintf` | Egress interface of the original direction |
| `dstinetsvc` | Internet Service name for the destination |
| `replysrcintf` / `replydstintf` | Interfaces of the reply direction |

```text
date=2026-10-28 time=09:21:10 eventtime=1793197270611038877 tz="-0500" logid="0000000013" type="traffic" subtype="forward" level="notice" vd="root" srcip=10.0.1.25 srcport=52114 srcintf="port3" srcintfrole="lan" dstip=198.51.100.80 dstport=443 dstintf="port2" dstintfrole="wan" sessionid=88412 proto=6 action="close" policyid=1 policytype="policy" service="HTTPS" trandisp="snat" transip=198.51.100.2 transport=52114 duration=31 sentbyte=18822 rcvdbyte=402114 vwlid=1 vwlname="Critical-DIA" vwlquality="Seq_num(2 port2), alive, sla(0x1), gid(0), cfg_order(1), cost(10), selected"
```

The session matched rule 1 `Critical-DIA` and left `port2` (member 2), which was alive and met SLA target 1. Seeing the higher-cost member in use is consistent with the event logs above: member 1 was out of SLA.

> **Exam tip:** If a session left the expected interface but the log has no `vwlid`/`vwlname`, it didn't match a configured SD-WAN rule (the Log Reference defines these fields as details of the matched rule). It was routed by the implicit rule (ECMP over the members' routes) or by a regular route or policy route. Confirm with a debug flow (no `Match policy routing` line) or the session's `sdwan_service_id`.

> **Watch out:** `vwlquality` is a free-text string (up to 320 characters) in the style of the rule's member line in `diagnose sys sdwan service4`. Its exact layout varies between builds and strategies (some builds add the zone name or print `local cost`), so read it for the sequence number, interface, alive/dead and `sla()` bits rather than memorising the format, and confirm the egress with `dstintf`.

Traffic logs only exist if the policy logs them. `set logtraffic all` logs every session; `set logtraffic utm` (the GUI's Security Events) logs only sessions with a security event; `disable` logs nothing, and you lose this evidence.

### Application steering and security logs

Application-based SD-WAN rules depend on application control learning which IP, protocol and port belong to an application; `diagnose sys sdwan internet-service-app-ctrl-list` shows what has been learned. The application control log for a session tells you the application was detected; the traffic log's `vwlservice` tells you an SD-WAN rule matched on it. If a new application's first sessions leave via the implicit rule, check whether it had been learned yet.

## Building a timeline

When a user reports "the app was slow at 09:20", line up the evidence:

1. **0113022923 Health Check** logs: did a member go out of SLA, and for which SLA target?
2. **0113022923 Service** logs: which rules reordered or dropped the member?
3. **0100022921**: did the member die completely (routes withdrawn)?
4. **0113022925** (if enabled): the measured latency, jitter and loss around that time.
5. **Traffic logs** for the user (`srcip`, `dstip`): `vwlname`, `vwlquality`, `dstintf` before and after.

## Lab it yourself

FortiGate-VM trial, LAN `port3`, WAN `port1`/`port2` in zone `underlay`, Linux router with netem in front of `port1`, disk or memory logging enabled:

1. Set `sla-fail-log-period 10` and `sla-pass-log-period 60` on `Internet_HC`.
2. Add 200 ms delay on the Linux router; collect the 0113022923 burst and the 0113022925 fail logs.
3. Add 100% loss; find the 0100022921 log under General System Events (not SD-WAN Events).
4. Browse from a LAN host and find `vwlid`/`vwlname`/`vwlquality` in the forward traffic log.

## Key takeaways

* 0100022921 Routing information changed: health check down/up, static routes removed or added; subtype **system**, level critical.
* 0113022923 SDWAN status: eventtype Health Check (in/out of SLA, number of pass members) or Service (member order, stop/start forwarding, load-balance set).
* 0113022924 SDWAN volume status: member over or under its volume/session ratio.
* 0113022925 SDWAN SLA information: periodic, only when `sla-fail-log-period` / `sla-pass-log-period` are non-zero (default 0).
* `member=` is the member sequence number; `seq="2,1"` is the new order; `slamap` is the SLA bitmap.
* Traffic logs: `vwlid`, `vwlname`, `vwlquality`, `vwlservice`; no SD-WAN fields means no configured SD-WAN rule matched (confirm with debug flow or the session list).
* Traffic evidence needs `logtraffic all` (or `utm` for security sessions only).
